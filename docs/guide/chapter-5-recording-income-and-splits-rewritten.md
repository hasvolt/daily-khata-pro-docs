# Chapter 5: Recording Income & Splits

## 5.1 Introduction

The **Recording Income & Splits** feature helps you maintain a clear
record of money received and, when Smart Fund Allocation is configured,
understand how that income could be divided across your chosen fund
categories.

Daily Khata Pro can be used to organize records for different kinds of
income, such as salary, freelance work, client payments, electrical
service work, business receipts, project payments, commissions, bonuses,
or other receipts. Keeping these entries consistent makes it easier to
review your financial activity later.

This chapter explains the general income-recording workflow, how
fund-split calculations work, how to check and correct entries, and how
to avoid common record-keeping mistakes.

**Important:** Daily Khata Pro is a record-keeping and planning tool.
Saving an income entry does not independently verify a payment with a
bank, employer, customer, or payment service. A fund split is a
calculation for organizing or planning money; it does not itself
transfer money between accounts.

The exact fields, labels, and available actions may vary by app version.

## 5.2 What Is an Income Record?

An income record is an entry you create to note money that you have
actually received. It can help answer questions such as:

-   How much money did I receive during a particular period?
-   Which client, employer, project, or business activity generated the
    payment?
-   When was the payment received?
-   What description will help me recognize the payment later?
-   How would the amount be distributed using my configured
    fund-allocation percentages?

Examples of money users may choose to record as income include:

-   Salary or wages.
-   Freelance or professional service payments.
-   Payments received against completed work.
-   Electrical service or installation payments.
-   Business sales receipts.
-   Project milestone payments.
-   Rental income.
-   Bonuses or commissions.
-   Other one-time receipts.

Choose a description that accurately reflects the transaction. Not every
incoming amount is necessarily earnings. A reimbursement, loan received,
refund, or transfer between your own accounts may need a separate
category or explanation, depending on the features available in your
version.

## 5.3 Received Income Versus Expected Income

One of the most important record-keeping habits is to distinguish
between money you have received and money you expect to receive.

For example:

-   A client has agreed to pay ₹5,000, but has not paid yet: this is an
    expected payment, not received income.
-   The client transfers ₹5,000 and you confirm that it has arrived: you
    can record the received amount.
-   You send an invoice: sending an invoice does not by itself mean the
    payment has been received.

If your version provides a separate feature for invoices, receivables,
or pending payments, use that feature according to its instructions. If
no such feature is available, keep expected payments separate from
received-income records so your totals do not become misleading.

## 5.5 How to Record an Income Entry

The following is a general workflow. The wording and placement of
controls can vary by version.

### Step 1: Open the income-entry area

Open the **Add** section or the area where new financial records are
created. Select the income-related option if it is available.

### Step 2: Enter the amount actually received

Enter the amount that has arrived. Review the digits and decimal places
before saving.

For example, ₹2,500 and ₹25,000 are very different amounts. If you
accidentally enter an extra zero or omit a digit, your records and any
allocation calculations may be incorrect.

Do not record an expected payment as received income unless your version
clearly provides a separate workflow for expected or pending amounts.

### Step 3: Identify the source

Select or describe the source using the options available in the app.
Examples include salary, client payment, project payment, business
receipt, or service payment.

Try to use consistent names. For example, if the same client pays you
several times, use a consistent client label rather than alternating
between several slightly different names.

### Step 4: Add a date, if the form supports it

Use the date appropriate to the record, usually the date the money was
received. If you are entering an older transaction later, check that the
date is correct before saving.

### Step 5: Select a payment method, if available

Choose the method that matches the actual payment, such as cash, bank
transfer, or UPI, if those choices are offered.

A selected payment method is a user-entered description. It does not
independently confirm that a transfer succeeded.

### Step 6: Add a useful description or reference

If a description field is available, write a short note that will make
sense when you see it again.

Useful examples:

-   `Salary — September`
-   `Electrical fault-finding visit`
-   `Website maintenance — Client A`
-   `Project milestone payment`
-   `Store sales — daily total`

Avoid putting passwords, app passcodes, UPI PINs, one-time passwords, or
full payment-card details in a transaction description.

### Step 7: Review the fund-split preview, if available

If Smart Fund Allocation is enabled and the app displays a proposed
split, review the categories, percentages, and calculated amounts before
completing the workflow.

The split depends on the income amount and the allocation settings
currently in use. If you recently changed those settings, the calculated
amounts may differ from an earlier entry or example.

### Step 8: Save the record

Use the available save or confirmation control. Wait for the action to
complete.

### Step 9: Verify the saved entry

If your version displays a confirmation, transaction list, balance
summary, or report, check that the entry appears as expected. Verify the
amount, date, source, and description where those details are shown.

If the entry does not appear, first check the list, search, filters, and
save status. Avoid repeatedly submitting the same entry without
checking, because that can create duplicates.

## 5.6 Understanding Smart Fund Allocation

Smart Fund Allocation can help you plan how a recorded amount is divided
among configured fund categories. The feature uses the percentages
entered in its settings to calculate each category's share.

The general calculation is:

`Allocated amount = Income amount × Allocation percentage ÷ 100`

For example, suppose you record **₹10,000** and have configured these
percentages:

  Fund category                 Allocation percentage   Calculated amount
  --------------------------- ----------------------- -------------------
  Savings                                         30%              ₹3,000
  Regular expenses                                50%              ₹5,000
  Emergency fund                                  10%              ₹1,000
  Personal or flexible fund                       10%              ₹1,000
  **Total**                                  **100%**         **₹10,000**

This is only an illustration. The names and percentages of your
categories depend on your own settings and the features available in
your version.

### What the split means

A calculated split shows how the entered income amount would be
allocated mathematically according to the configured percentages. It
does not necessarily mean that money has been moved to separate bank
accounts, wallets, or investment products.

If you use separate accounts or physical envelopes, you are responsible
for carrying out any real-world movement of money yourself unless your
installed version explicitly documents a separate integration that
performs such an action.

## 5.7 More Fund-Split Examples

### Example A: Income of ₹25,000

Suppose the configured percentages are 20% for savings, 50% for regular
expenses, 20% for an emergency fund, and 10% for a flexible category.

  Fund category         Percentage   Calculated amount
  ------------------- ------------ -------------------
  Savings                      20%              ₹5,000
  Regular expenses             50%             ₹12,500
  Emergency fund               20%              ₹5,000
  Flexible category            10%              ₹2,500
  **Total**               **100%**         **₹25,000**

### Example B: Income of ₹18,000

Suppose the allocation is 40% for regular expenses, 25% for savings, 15%
for an emergency fund, and 20% for a flexible category.

  Fund category         Percentage   Calculated amount
  ------------------- ------------ -------------------
  Regular expenses             40%              ₹7,200
  Savings                      25%              ₹4,500
  Emergency fund               15%              ₹2,700
  Flexible category            20%              ₹3,600
  **Total**               **100%**         **₹18,000**

### Example C: Allocation percentages total less than 100%

If you configure categories totalling 90%, the calculated categories
account for 90% of the entered amount. For an income of ₹10,000, that
represents ₹9,000, leaving ₹1,000 outside those configured percentages.

Whether your app displays a remainder or handles this situation in
another way depends on its implementation. Review the settings and any
total shown by the app before relying on the result.

### Example D: Allocation percentages total more than 100%

If the percentages total 110%, the calculated categories represent more
than the original income amount. For an income of ₹10,000, 110%
corresponds to ₹11,000.

Review and correct the percentages if the intended plan is to distribute
the full income amount without exceeding it. Do not assume that the
application will automatically adjust the percentages unless your
version explicitly indicates that it does.

## 5.8 Checking Allocation Percentages

If the app expects fund categories to represent the whole income amount,
the percentages should total **100%**.

Before relying on an allocation preview:

1.  Review every configured category.
2.  Check each percentage for typing errors.
3.  Add the percentages together.
4.  Confirm that the total matches your intended allocation.
5.  Review the calculated amounts shown by the app, if available.
6.  Consider rounding differences for amounts that do not divide evenly.

Do not assume that the categories or percentages in an example are
recommended for your personal circumstances. Choose settings that suit
your own needs and review them when your circumstances change.

## 5.9 Rounding and Small Differences

Percentage calculations can produce fractional amounts. Depending on how
the application displays currency, a calculated value may be rounded to
two decimal places or to a whole currency unit.

For example, a percentage calculation may produce a value such as
₹333.333.... The displayed amount may be rounded, and the sum of
individually rounded categories can sometimes differ slightly from the
original amount.

If the app displays a total, remainder, or rounding adjustment, review
it. For formal accounts, taxes, invoices, or business reporting, verify
figures independently rather than assuming a planning split is an
accounting statement.

## 5.10 Using Consistent Income Sources

Consistent descriptions make records easier to understand and may
improve searching or filtering where those features are available.

  Situation             Example description
  --------------------- -------------------------------------------
  Monthly salary        `Salary — September 2026`
  Freelance service     `Website maintenance — Client A`
  Electrical service    `Site inspection — Job 104`
  Installation work     `Light installation — Customer reference`
  Business receipts     `Store sales — 29 September`
  Project work          `Project milestone — Phase 1`
  Bonus or commission   `Quarterly incentive`

These are examples, not required categories. Use only fields and options
that your version supports.

For recurring payments, a consistent format can help you compare entries
across months. If you use client or project references, make sure the
reference is meaningful to you without exposing unnecessary confidential
information.

## 5.11 Editing an Income Entry

If the application provides an edit option, use it to correct a record
that contains an error.

1.  Open the relevant record.
2.  Check its date, amount, source, and description to confirm that it
    is the correct entry.
3.  Change only the details that need correction.
4.  Save the changes using the available control.
5.  Recheck the saved record.
6.  Review any affected balance, allocation preview, or report that your
    version provides.

Changing the amount may change the calculated fund split and summaries
that depend on the record. A change to the allocation settings may also
affect the preview shown later.

If a field cannot be edited in your version, use the correction workflow
that the app supports. Do not assume every field or record can be
changed in place.

## 5.12 Correcting Duplicate or Mistaken Entries

Mistakes can happen when entering records. Before making a correction,
identify the exact entry and check how your version handles editing,
deletion, and re-entry.

Recommended checks:

-   Compare the date, amount, source, and description.
-   Confirm whether the entry is genuinely duplicated.
-   Avoid deleting a similar but valid transaction.
-   Use the app's supported correction process.
-   Verify the corrected record after saving.
-   Review relevant totals or reports.
-   Keep an independent backup before making major changes, where backup
    options are available.

If you are unsure whether an entry was saved, inspect the records list
first rather than submitting the same entry again.

A saved record is maintained by the user. It is not independently
verified against a bank account, employer, customer, or payment
provider.

## 5.13 Reviewing Income Records and Reports

After recording income, use the available **Records**, **History**, or
**Reports** areas to review your entries. The exact sections and
functions depend on your app version.

Where supported, you may be able to review:

-   Individual income records.
-   Dates and descriptions.
-   Income totals for a selected period.
-   Records grouped or filtered by category or source.
-   Income alongside expenses.
-   Fund-allocation calculations.
-   Printable or exportable summaries.

When reviewing a total, check the selected date range and any active
filters. A total can differ from your expectation if some entries fall
outside the period, a filter hides them, or an entry was duplicated,
edited, or removed.

Reports depend on the quality of the underlying data. Review important
entries before sharing a report or using it for formal business
purposes.

## 5.14 Privacy and Sensitive Information

Income records may reveal details about personal finances, clients,
employment, and business activity. Keep descriptions useful but avoid
including information that is not needed to identify the transaction.

Do not enter the following in a transaction description or note:

-   App passcodes or security answers.
-   Bank passwords, UPI PINs, or one-time passwords.
-   Full payment-card details.
-   Unnecessary identity-document details.
-   Confidential client information unrelated to the payment.

Use the app's security options where available, protect access to your
device, and follow the relevant backup and data-safety instructions. Do
not assume that a passcode or a private-looking screen means that every
stored record is encrypted; check the app's security documentation for
the actual protections implemented in your version.

## 5.15 Common Problems and Troubleshooting

### Problem: The fund split looks incorrect

Check the entered income amount, the configured percentages, and the
total percentage. Also consider rounding. If you recently changed
allocation settings, confirm which settings are currently being used.

### Problem: The income entry is missing

Check whether the save action completed. Review the Records or History
area and check any active search terms or filters. Do not immediately
submit the same entry again.

### Problem: The same income appears more than once

Compare the records carefully and identify which entry is duplicated.
Correct the duplicate using the available edit or delete workflow, then
review totals again.

### Problem: The income total differs from expectations

Check the selected date range, filters, dates, amounts, duplicate
records, and any entries that were edited or removed. Make sure expected
payments have not been included as received income.

### Problem: An expected payment has not arrived

Do not record it as received income. If your version supports pending
payments or receivables, use that feature; otherwise, keep a separate
note until the payment is received.

### Problem: A fund category shows an unexpected amount

Check the original income amount and the category's configured
percentage. Review the allocation settings and any rounding applied to
displayed values.

### Problem: A record was saved with the wrong date or amount

Open the correct record and use the supported editing or correction
process. After correcting it, verify the record and any totals or
reports that may have changed.

## 5.16 Good Record-Keeping Practices

For clearer and more dependable records:

-   Record income soon after the payment is received.
-   Enter the actual amount, not an estimate.
-   Check the date and amount before saving.
-   Use consistent source names and descriptions.
-   Separate received income from pending payments.
-   Review allocation percentages before relying on a split.
-   Check the total allocation when the categories are intended to cover
    the whole amount.
-   Review records regularly for duplicates or typing errors.
-   Keep backups using the app's supported backup options.
-   Verify exported or printed figures before sharing them.
-   Avoid storing unnecessary confidential information in descriptions.
-   Recheck important totals before using them for business or formal
    purposes.

## 5.17 Important Limitations and Disclaimer

Daily Khata Pro is intended to help organize financial records and
perform calculations based on information entered by the user.

-   An income entry does not itself prove that money was received.
-   A payment method selected in a record does not independently verify
    a transaction.
-   A fund-allocation preview is a mathematical calculation, not
    necessarily a transfer of money.
-   Allocation percentages do not guarantee savings, investment returns,
    or financial outcomes.
-   Reports can be inaccurate if the underlying records are incomplete
    or incorrect.
-   The available fields, categories, reports, editing tools, and backup
    options depend on the implemented features and the installed
    version.

Do not treat an allocation preview or personal record as a substitute
for bank statements, formal accounting records, tax advice, or
professional financial advice where those are required.

## 5.19 Chapter Summary

The **Recording Income & Splits** feature helps you document money
received and, when Smart Fund Allocation is configured, calculate a
proposed distribution across fund categories.

A careful workflow is to record only money actually received, enter an
accurate amount and date, identify the source clearly, add a useful
reference where supported, review the allocation, save the entry, and
verify the result. Consistent descriptions, correct percentages, regular
reviews, and secure backups make the resulting records more useful.
