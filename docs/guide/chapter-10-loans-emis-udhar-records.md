# Chapter 10: Loans, EMIs & Udhar Records

## 10.1 Overview

The **Loans, EMIs & Udhar Records** section is intended to help users
organize information about money they have borrowed, money they have
lent to others, and repayments related to those arrangements.

Borrowing and lending can involve several dates, partial payments,
agreed repayment terms, and supporting documents. Keeping clear records
can help you review what happened, what has been repaid, and what amount
may remain according to the entries you have recorded.

This chapter explains common debt-record concepts, practical workflows,
examples, accuracy checks, and privacy considerations. The exact fields,
actions, calculations, reminders, and reports available depend on the
installed version of Daily Khata Pro. Follow only the options that are
actually present in your application.

> **Important:** A record in Daily Khata Pro is a personal tracking
> entry. It is not, by itself, a legal agreement, proof of payment, bank
> confirmation, or guarantee that a borrower or lender will fulfil an
> arrangement.

## 10.2 Understanding Loans, EMIs and Udhar

These terms are related, but they describe different situations.
Understanding the difference helps prevent records from being entered in
the wrong direction.

### 10.2.1 Money borrowed

Money borrowed is money you receive from another person, a bank, a
financial institution, or another lender with an obligation to repay it
under the applicable agreement.

Examples include: - Borrowing money from a relative for a planned
expense. - Taking a formal personal loan. - Borrowing money for
equipment, education, travel, or household needs. - Receiving a
temporary advance that must later be returned.

Borrowed money should not automatically be treated as income or profit.
It may increase the cash available to you, but it also creates an
obligation to repay. The way it appears in your personal records depends
on the application's accounting model and your bookkeeping approach.

### 10.2.2 Money lent (Udhar given)

Money lent is money you give to another person with an understanding
that it will be returned.

Examples include: - Lending money to a friend or family member. - Giving
a temporary advance to a colleague. - Recording an amount a customer or
another person is expected to return, where that arrangement is
appropriate to track.

Money lent is not the same as cash currently available to spend. Until
the repayment is actually received, keep the outstanding amount distinct
from money you can use immediately.

### 10.2.3 EMI (Equated Monthly Instalment)

An EMI is a scheduled periodic payment commonly used for formal loans.
Depending on the loan agreement, an instalment may include both
principal repayment and interest.

The EMI amount, due date, interest rate, fees, payment allocation, and
repayment conditions should be checked against the lender's official
documents. Do not assume that a personal tracking record calculates the
lender's official outstanding balance.

### 10.2.4 Repayment made or received

A repayment is money paid toward an amount you owe, or money received
from someone who owes you.

A repayment should be recorded as completed only when it has actually
occurred. A promise, reminder, planned transfer, or scheduled instalment
is not proof that a payment was completed.

## 10.3 What Can Be Tracked?

Where supported by the installed application, debt records may help
organize information such as:

-   Whether money was borrowed or lent.
-   A name or label identifying the other party.
-   The original principal amount.
-   The transaction date.
-   Repayments made or received.
-   A remaining amount calculated from recorded entries.
-   An agreed due date or repayment schedule.
-   Notes describing the arrangement.
-   Related reference information, where appropriate.

The actual fields and actions may differ by version. If a field,
reminder, report, or action is not available in the current version, do
not assume that the application stores or calculates that information.

A record is only as complete as the information entered. If an important
term is not represented in the app, keep the supporting information
separately.

## 10.4 Before Creating a Debt Record

Before adding a loan or udhar entry, gather the details you can verify.

1.  **Direction:** Is this money you borrowed, or money you lent to
    someone?
2.  **Amount:** Confirm the principal amount using a receipt, transfer
    record, message, written note, or other reliable source.
3.  **Date:** Record the actual transaction date as accurately as
    possible.
4.  **Other party:** Use a clear name or label so that different people
    or arrangements are not confused.
5.  **Repayment terms:** Note the agreed schedule or due date if
    relevant and supported by the application.
6.  **Interest or fees:** Check the actual agreement. Do not invent or
    assume a rate.
7.  **Supporting information:** Keep receipts, transfer confirmations,
    statements, or written agreements separately when appropriate.
8.  **Existing records:** Check whether the same arrangement has already
    been entered.

Avoid entering sensitive information that is not necessary for your
records, such as passwords, PINs, full payment-card details,
authentication codes, or account login credentials.

## 10.5 Recording Money Borrowed

If the application provides a way to record borrowed money, use the
available fields to describe the transaction accurately.

### General workflow

1.  Open the relevant loan or debt-record feature.
2.  Choose the borrowed-money or equivalent record type, if available.
3.  Enter the verified amount and transaction date.
4.  Add a clear lender label or description, if supported.
5.  Record the agreed due date or repayment details only if known and
    supported.
6.  Review the entry carefully before saving.
7.  After saving, confirm that the record appears as expected.
8.  Keep any relevant lender statement or transfer confirmation
    separately.

### Example

Suppose you receive ₹15,000 from a relative and agree to repay it later.

Your personal record might identify: - Amount borrowed: ₹15,000. -
Transaction date: the date you actually received the money. - Lender
label: a clear name or description. - Repayment terms: only the terms
you have actually agreed. - Supporting note: any useful non-sensitive
context.

This example illustrates record organization, not a recommended
borrowing arrangement.

A record of borrowed money should not automatically be treated as income
or profit. Borrowed money creates an obligation to repay according to
the relevant agreement. Check how the application represents the entry
before interpreting its effect on balances or reports.

## 10.6 Recording Money Lent to Someone

If the application supports recording money lent to another person, use
the available controls to record the amount that was actually given.

### General workflow

1.  Open the relevant loan, udhar, or debt-record feature.
2.  Choose the lent-money or equivalent record type, if available.
3.  Enter the amount actually given.
4.  Record the transaction date.
5.  Add a clear label for the person or arrangement, if supported.
6.  Add an agreed repayment date or note only when it is accurate.
7.  Review the details and save if the feature is available.
8.  Keep a separate receipt, transfer confirmation, or written agreement
    when appropriate.

### Example

Suppose you lend ₹8,000 to a person. Your record should distinguish this
amount from money you currently hold or have already received back.

If you later receive a repayment, record that repayment against the
correct arrangement if the application supports repayment tracking. Do
not reduce the outstanding amount merely because the other person has
promised to pay.

A personal record can help you remember the arrangement, but it does not
independently establish that the other person accepts the amount or the
repayment terms. Retain relevant supporting information when
appropriate.

## 10.7 Tracking Repayments

Repayments change the amount outstanding, so accurate records are
important.

Before recording a repayment, confirm:

-   The correct debt or loan entry is selected.
-   The amount was actually paid or received.
-   The transaction date is correct.
-   The repayment is assigned to the correct arrangement.
-   The entry has not already been recorded.
-   Any reference or note accurately describes the payment.

If the application provides repayment tracking, follow its displayed
workflow and review the updated balance afterwards. If repayments are
entered as separate records rather than linked automatically, make sure
you understand how those entries affect your overall totals.

### 10.7.1 Example: Repayment on money lent

Suppose you lend ₹5,000 to a person and later receive a repayment of
₹1,500.

  Detail                                          Amount
  --------------------------------------------- --------
  Original amount lent                            ₹5,000
  Repayment actually received                     ₹1,500
  Remaining amount in this simplified example     ₹3,500

Calculation:

`₹5,000 − ₹1,500 = ₹3,500`

The remaining amount is ₹3,500 in this simplified example, before
considering any separately agreed interest or adjustments. This
calculation does not establish that the other person agrees with the
balance; compare it with your supporting records and the agreed terms.

### 10.7.2 Example: Partial repayment on borrowed money

Suppose you borrow ₹10,000 and later repay ₹2,000 of the principal.

  Detail                                              Amount
  ------------------------------------------------ ---------
  Original principal                                 ₹10,000
  Principal repaid                                    ₹2,000
  Remaining principal in this simplified example      ₹8,000

Calculation:

`₹10,000 − ₹2,000 = ₹8,000`

An actual loan balance may differ if interest, fees, charges, or
lender-specific repayment rules apply. Use the lender's official
statement for the authoritative amount due.

### 10.7.3 Partial payments and multiple repayments

If a debt is repaid in several instalments, record each completed
payment accurately where the application supports this workflow. For
example, three separate repayments should not be combined into a single
completed payment unless that matches how your records are intended to
be maintained.

After each repayment: - Check the amount and date. - Verify the correct
debt entry. - Review the updated outstanding figure, if displayed. -
Keep confirmation of the actual payment where appropriate. - Avoid
entering the same payment twice.

## 10.8 Understanding EMIs and Due Dates

If you have an EMI or recurring repayment obligation, use the lender's
schedule as the primary reference.

Review the following details where relevant:

-   Instalment amount.
-   Due date.
-   Loan account or reference label, without exposing unnecessary
    sensitive details.
-   Whether a payment is scheduled, pending, or completed.
-   Payment confirmation or receipt.
-   Any fees or changes communicated by the lender.
-   The current outstanding amount shown by the lender.

If Daily Khata Pro supports due-date notes or reminders, configure them
using the options actually available. Do not assume a reminder has been
created unless the application confirms it.

A reminder is only a prompt to review or make a payment. It does not
automatically pay an EMI, contact the lender, or guarantee that a
payment will be processed. Always verify payment completion through the
relevant bank, payment service, or lender confirmation.

### Example: Keeping an EMI record

Suppose your lender's official schedule shows an instalment due on a
particular date. You may keep a personal reference record containing the
instalment amount, due date, and a non-sensitive loan label if those
fields are supported.

After making the payment, update the record only when the payment has
actually completed. If the payment is pending, failed, reversed, or
still processing, do not mark it as completed without checking its
status.

## 10.9 Interest, Fees and Outstanding Balances

A simple subtraction of repayments from the original amount may not
represent the official outstanding balance of a formal loan.

The actual amount owed can depend on:

-   Interest and how it is calculated.
-   The timing of each payment.
-   Fees, penalties, or other charges under the agreement.
-   Whether a payment is allocated to interest, principal, or both.
-   Late-payment rules or other conditions.
-   Changes or adjustments made by the lender.
-   Whether any payment was reversed or not successfully processed.

For example, if a loan has interest or fees, subtracting the total EMI
amount from the original principal may produce an incorrect principal
balance. An EMI may include interest as well as principal repayment.

For formal loans, check the latest lender statement or contact the
lender if an amount is unclear. Use Daily Khata Pro as a personal
tracking aid unless the installed version explicitly supports the
required calculation and you have verified its method.

Do not rely on an estimated balance as the sole basis for a formal
payment or financial decision.

## 10.10 Distinguishing Loan Records from Income and Expenses

Borrowed money, money lent, repayments, and everyday income or expenses
describe different financial events. Recording them incorrectly can make
summaries harder to interpret.

Consider these distinctions:

-   **Borrowing money:** You receive funds, but an obligation to repay
    may exist.
-   **Lending money:** You give funds to another party, and repayment
    may be expected.
-   **Receiving repayment:** Money previously lent is returned, in whole
    or in part.
-   **Making repayment:** You return money you previously borrowed.
-   **Interest or fees:** These are separate terms of an arrangement and
    should be recorded according to your bookkeeping method and the
    application's supported fields.
-   **Everyday income or expense:** These generally describe earnings or
    costs, not necessarily the movement of loan principal.

The correct way to represent these events depends on the application's
accounting model and your personal bookkeeping approach. Do not assume a
loan entry automatically changes a particular fund, category, or report
in a specific way unless the application explicitly shows that behavior.

## 10.11 Avoiding Duplicate or Incorrect Entries

Duplicate records can make an outstanding balance appear larger or
smaller than it really is. Incorrect direction, date, or amount can also
make later reviews confusing.

To reduce mistakes:

-   Check existing records before adding a new loan or repayment.
-   Do not record the same repayment twice.
-   Distinguish a new loan from a repayment on an existing loan.
-   Review the amount and direction of every transaction.
-   Confirm the date against the actual transaction.
-   If you correct an entry, check the resulting balance afterwards.
-   Keep supporting receipts or statements for comparison.

If a record appears incorrect, review the original transaction details
before editing or deleting it. If the application has a recycle bin,
history, or recovery option, use it only as described by the current
interface. Do not assume deleted entries can always be restored.

## 10.12 Privacy and Responsible Record-Keeping

Debt records can contain sensitive personal and financial information.
Protect them carefully.

Recommended practices: - Use a device lock and any available application
security features. - Avoid putting full bank-account numbers, passwords,
PINs, or authentication codes in notes. - Do not share screenshots or
exported reports without checking what information is visible. - Keep
independent backups if the application supports export or backup. -
Store backup files in a safe location. - Understand that locally stored
data may be lost if application or browser storage is cleared, the
device is lost, or the data becomes corrupted. - Verify a backup before
relying on it. - Retain supporting agreements, receipts, transfer
confirmations, and lender statements when appropriate.

Do not assume that an app passcode or private display feature encrypts
every record or protects exported copies. Review the application's
current security and backup information to understand the protection
available in your version.

## 10.13 Common Problems and Checks

### The outstanding amount looks incorrect

Review the original amount, all repayments, the direction of each entry,
and any applicable interest or fees. For formal loans, compare the
result with the lender's latest statement.

### A repayment is missing

Check whether it was recorded under the correct debt entry and whether
the payment actually completed. A scheduled or promised payment should
not be marked as received or paid unless that is accurate.

### A balance appears to have changed unexpectedly

Review recent edits, duplicate transactions, and repayment entries. If
the application's calculation is unclear, keep a separate verified
record while you investigate.

### A due date or reminder is not visible

Check whether the installed version supports due dates or reminders and
whether the relevant option was saved. Do not assume the feature is
active if no confirmation is shown.

### A record was entered in the wrong direction

Review whether the entry represents money borrowed or money lent.
Correct it carefully using the options available in the application, and
verify the resulting totals.

### A payment was pending, reversed, or failed

Check the payment service or lender's status before changing the record.
Do not treat a pending or failed payment as a completed repayment.

### A feature described here is not available

The interface and feature set can vary by version. Use the controls
shown in your installed version and consult the relevant application
information rather than assuming a missing feature exists.

## 10.14 Good Practices for Managing Loans and Udhar

-   Record a transaction as soon as practical after it occurs.
-   Use clear labels to distinguish different people and arrangements.
-   Verify amounts and dates against reliable records.
-   Keep borrowed money, money lent, and repayments conceptually
    separate.
-   Record only completed repayments as completed.
-   Check formal EMI amounts and due dates against lender documents.
-   Review outstanding entries periodically.
-   Avoid unnecessary sensitive information in notes.
-   Keep receipts, statements, and agreements when appropriate.
-   Maintain independent backups of important records.
-   Resolve disagreements by comparing records and communicating with
    the other party.
-   Do not rely on an app entry alone as proof of a legal agreement or
    completed payment.

## 10.15 Important Notice

Daily Khata Pro is intended to assist with personal financial
organization and record-keeping. A debt entry is not a legal agreement,
proof of payment, lender statement, or guarantee of repayment. The
accuracy of any displayed total depends on the information entered and
the calculation method used by the application.

Formal loan terms, interest, fees, repayment schedules, and amounts due
should be verified against the relevant lender's official documents.
Users are responsible for checking their records and protecting their
financial information. Available functionality may vary by application
version.

This chapter explains record-keeping concepts and does not provide
individualized financial, legal, tax, or lending advice.
