# Chapter 12: Reports, Charts & PDF Statements

## Overview

Reports, charts, and statements help you review the financial
information recorded in Daily Khata Pro without opening every
transaction individually. Depending on the features available in your
installed version, a report may bring together income, expenses,
category totals, balances, allocations, or activity recorded during a
selected period.

This chapter explains how to interpret reports, review figures, prepare
an export, and keep report files organized. The exact names and
locations of buttons may vary by version. Follow only the controls that
are actually available in your application.

> **Feature availability:** Date filters, charts, PDF statements, CSV or
> spreadsheet export, print controls, and specific report types may not
> be available in every version. The examples in this chapter explain
> general concepts; they do not guarantee that a particular control
> exists in your installation.

## 12.1 What Are Financial Reports?

A financial report organizes recorded information into a readable
summary. Instead of reviewing transactions one by one, you can use a
report to review a period, understand recorded activity, or prepare
information for your own reference.

Depending on the application's available reporting features, a report
may summarize:

-   Income and incoming payments
-   Expenses and deductions
-   Category-wise spending
-   Balances or financial allocations
-   Transactions within a selected period
-   Other record types supported by the application

### How reports use your records

A report is normally generated from the information entered into the
application. It does not independently know whether you forgot to record
a cash purchase, entered a payment twice, or selected the wrong
category.

For example, if you record three expenses but forget to enter a fourth
expense, the report can only summarize the three recorded entries. The
total may be mathematically correct while still being incomplete
compared with your real-world activity.

### When reports are useful

Reports can support:

-   Personal monthly reviews
-   Household expense reviews
-   Freelance or work-related record keeping
-   Small-business internal tracking
-   Reviewing category spending
-   Preparing a reference copy for your own records
-   Finding entries that may need correction

Reports are organizational tools. They are not automatically an audit,
official bank statement, tax filing, or professional accounting service.

## 12.2 Why Use Reports?

Reports can help you:

1.  Review financial activity over a chosen period.
2.  Compare recorded income with recorded expenses.
3.  Understand which categories account for recorded spending.
4.  Review entries before preparing a statement.
5.  Keep a readable archive of financial activity.
6.  Export supported information for further review.
7.  Notice missing, duplicated, or incorrectly categorized records.
8.  Compare periods, provided the reports use equivalent filters and
    definitions.

A report is most useful when your records are entered consistently. A
quick review before relying on a total can prevent misunderstandings.

## 12.3 Understanding Income and Expense Summaries

An income summary groups money received and recorded during a period. An
expense summary groups money spent and recorded during that period.

A simple comparison is:

`Net Difference = Total Recorded Income - Total Recorded Expenses`

### Example

Suppose the records for one month show:

  Item                            Amount
  ------------------------- ------------
  Total recorded income          ₹25,000
  Total recorded expenses        ₹18,000
  **Difference**              **₹7,000**

The calculation is:

`₹25,000 - ₹18,000 = ₹7,000`

This is a difference between the income and expenses included in the
summary. Do not automatically interpret it as your bank balance, cash in
hand, business profit, or money currently available to spend.

### Why the difference may not equal available money

Other factors may affect your real financial position, such as:

-   Opening balances from an earlier period
-   Transfers between your own accounts or fund categories
-   Money borrowed or lent
-   Loan principal repayments and interest
-   Pending payments or expected income
-   Transactions that were not recorded
-   Transactions included in another report or date range
-   Rules the application uses to include or exclude particular record
    types

### Review these points before interpreting a summary

-   Is the date range correct?
-   Which record types are included?
-   Are transfers or allocations included, excluded, or shown
    separately?
-   Are any transactions missing or duplicated?
-   Does the displayed figure represent a total, a difference, or a
    balance?
-   Are you comparing the same type of figure across both periods?

When the meaning of a displayed total is unclear, consult the labels and
explanations in the current interface rather than assuming it represents
a bank balance or profit.

## 12.4 Understanding Category-Wise Reports

A category-wise report groups transactions according to their assigned
categories. It can help you see how recorded spending is distributed
across groceries, transport, household bills, dining, work costs, or
other categories you use.

### Illustrative example

  Expense category     Recorded amount
  ------------------ -----------------
  Groceries                     ₹4,200
  Transport                     ₹1,500
  Household bills               ₹2,800
  Dining                        ₹1,100
  Other expenses                  ₹900
  **Total**                **₹10,500**

These figures are an example only. Your totals depend on your own
entries and the categories used in your version of the application.

### How to read category totals

1.  Check the report period.
2.  Review the category names and their recorded amounts.
3.  Confirm that similar transactions have been assigned consistently.
4.  Investigate any unusually high or low category total.
5.  Compare the sum of the categories with the report total, if both are
    displayed.

### Why category consistency matters

Imagine that electricity bills are sometimes entered under **Household
Bills** and sometimes under **Other Expenses**. The overall expense
total might still be correct, but neither category will show the
complete amount for that type of spending.

Use clear, consistent category names. If the application supports
category editing, review the available options before changing
categories on existing records. A category change may affect how reports
group those records.

## 12.5 Understanding Charts and Visual Summaries

If charts are available in your version, they may present financial
information visually. Depending on the chart type, they may show
comparisons between categories, changes over time, or relationships
between totals.

Charts can be helpful for a quick overview, but check the underlying
figures when accuracy matters.

### What to check in a chart

-   **Title:** What information is the chart presenting?
-   **Date range:** Which dates are included?
-   **Labels:** What does each category or section represent?
-   **Units:** Does the chart show rupee amounts, percentages, or
    counts?
-   **Totals:** Do the displayed figures match the corresponding report
    or records?
-   **Filters:** Is the chart using the same categories and transaction
    types as the table you are comparing it with?

### Avoid reading too much into visual differences

A chart can make a category look much larger than another because of the
scale or the way the chart is presented. Small differences may be hard
to judge visually. Use the numeric values when comparing exact amounts.

A chart is a visual explanation of the selected data; it does not
independently verify that the records are complete or correct.

## 12.6 Selecting a Date Range

If date filters are available, select a period that matches the purpose
of your review. Common periods include a single day, a week, a month, or
a custom range.

### General workflow

1.  Open the relevant report or summary area.
2.  Locate the date filter, if the current interface provides one.
3.  Select the required start and end dates.
4.  Apply or confirm the selection if the interface requires it.
5.  Check that the report reflects the intended period.
6.  Review the included records before exporting or printing.

### Example: reviewing September

If you want to review September 2026, confirm that the selected range
includes the intended first and last dates of that month. Do not assume
that a preset called "Last 30 days" is the same as a calendar month.

### Comparing two periods

For a useful comparison:

-   Use equivalent date ranges where possible.
-   Confirm that both reports include the same types of transactions.
-   Use the same category definitions.
-   Check whether one period contains incomplete or unentered records.
-   Be aware of differences in the number of days when comparing months.

A higher total in one period does not automatically explain why the
total changed. Review the underlying entries before drawing conclusions.

## 12.7 Reviewing a Report Before Exporting

Before saving, printing, or sharing a report, review its contents
carefully.

### Review checklist

-   The report type is the one you intended to open.
-   The date range is correct.
-   Income and expense totals appear reasonable.
-   Category names are clear and consistent.
-   Important transactions are present.
-   Duplicate or incorrectly entered transactions have been reviewed.
-   Currency and number formatting are understandable.
-   The report does not include private notes or unrelated information
    unintentionally.
-   The displayed figures have the meaning you expect.

### If a figure looks incorrect

Do not change a report manually simply to make a total appear correct.
Review the underlying transactions and identify the reason for the
difference. Correct the original entry only when you understand what was
recorded incorrectly and the application supports editing it.

Keep in mind that a report can be internally consistent while still
missing transactions that were never entered.

## 12.8 PDF Statements

If PDF statement generation is available, a PDF can provide a portable
document for printing, archiving, or sharing. The contents and
appearance depend on the application's implemented export feature.

### General PDF workflow

1.  Open the report or statement you want to save.
2.  Select the date range or report options, if available.
3.  Review the displayed information before exporting.
4.  Choose the PDF, print, or export option if present.
5.  Follow the save or print prompts shown by your device or browser.
6.  Open the saved file.
7.  Check that the pages, labels, and figures are readable.

The exact workflow may differ between devices, browsers, and application
versions.

### Check the saved PDF

Before relying on it, confirm that:

-   The correct period is included.
-   All expected pages are present.
-   Tables and columns are not cut off.
-   Text and amounts are readable.
-   Totals match the report you reviewed.
-   Currency symbols and special characters display properly.
-   The file does not expose information you did not intend to share.

If the PDF is incomplete, return to the report and check the selected
period and available export or print settings. Avoid assuming that the
saved document contains every record in the application.

### Important distinction

Do not treat an application-generated PDF as an official tax document,
bank statement, audited account, or legally validated financial
statement unless it has been specifically prepared and validated for
that purpose.

## 12.9 CSV and Spreadsheet Exports

If CSV or spreadsheet export is supported, the exported data may be
opened in a compatible spreadsheet application for additional review.

A CSV file generally stores tabular information as rows and columns of
text separated by delimiters. It may not preserve the formatting,
charts, page layout, or styling of a formatted report.

### General export workflow

1.  Open the relevant report or data view.
2.  Select the supported export option, if available.
3.  Save the file in a location you can find again.
4.  Open it with a compatible spreadsheet or text application.
5.  Check that columns are separated correctly.
6.  Verify dates, amounts, categories, and totals.
7.  Confirm that currency symbols and special characters display
    correctly.
8.  Keep an unchanged reference copy if you plan to edit a separate
    working copy.

### Spreadsheet interpretation issues

Spreadsheet applications may interpret dates, decimal separators, long
numbers, and text values differently. For example, a date may display in
a different format after import. This does not necessarily mean the
original record changed, but it should be checked before using the data.

When importing a CSV, use the spreadsheet application's import settings
if available. Verify important values before sorting, editing, or
sharing the imported data.

### Export is not automatically a full backup

A report export may include only selected fields or a chosen date range.
It may not include settings, private notes, all record types, or
information required to restore the application. See the separate backup
and restoration instructions for data-safety guidance.

## 12.10 Printing and Archiving Statements

If printing is supported through the report interface or your device's
print function, review the print preview before completing the job.

### Printing checklist

-   Select the intended page size and orientation.
-   Confirm that all required pages are included.
-   Check that tables and amounts are not cut off.
-   Ensure that text remains readable.
-   Avoid printing unnecessary private information.
-   Store paper statements securely.

If the print preview is difficult to read, check the available layout or
scaling options. The exact settings depend on the device, browser, and
printing software.

### Digital archive recommendations

Use descriptive file names that identify the report type and period
without revealing unnecessary sensitive information.

Example:

`monthly-summary-2026-09.pdf`

This is more useful than a generic name such as `file1.pdf`. If you keep
multiple versions, use clear version labels and avoid accidentally
replacing the only copy you need.

Keep exported reports in a location protected by your device's security
features. Remember that files copied to another folder, device, or cloud
service may have different access and privacy protections.

## 12.11 Sharing Reports Safely

Financial reports may contain sensitive information, including income,
expenses, balances, business activity, and details about other people.

Before sharing a report:

1.  Confirm that the recipient needs the information.
2.  Review every page and section.
3.  Remove or exclude unrelated private information where possible.
4.  Avoid creating public links to files containing financial details.
5.  Use appropriate device, account, and file-sharing security features.
6.  Delete temporary copies from shared devices when appropriate.
7.  Check the destination and recipient before sending the file.

Exporting creates another copy of the information. Any privacy
protections provided by the application may not apply to a file once it
has been saved, copied, printed, or shared elsewhere.

If the report contains information about clients, customers, employees,
family members, or other people, consider whether you have permission to
share it and whether all included details are necessary.

## 12.12 Common Reporting Problems

### A report total looks wrong

Check the selected date range, included transaction types, categories,
and underlying entries. Look for missing, duplicated, or incorrectly
recorded transactions. Also check whether the displayed figure is a
total, difference, or balance.

### A transaction is missing from a report

Confirm its date, category, and record type. Check whether it falls
within the selected period and whether the report's filters exclude that
type of record. If the application has different views for different
record types, make sure you are reviewing the relevant view.

### A chart and a table appear different

Verify that both views use the same date range, categories, units, and
transaction filters. A chart may show percentages while a table shows
currency amounts. If the difference remains unexplained, review the
underlying records rather than relying on visual estimates.

### A PDF is blank or incomplete

First confirm that the report itself contains data. Repeat the available
export or print workflow and open the saved file in a compatible PDF
viewer if necessary. Check the date range and any selected report
options. Exact troubleshooting steps depend on the device and
application version.

### A CSV file opens with incorrect columns

The spreadsheet application may have interpreted the delimiter or
character encoding differently. Use the import options available in that
application, then verify the result before editing or sharing the data.

### Dates or currency values display differently after import

Check the spreadsheet's date, number, and regional settings. Compare
important values against the original report or records. Do not assume a
display-format difference means the underlying value is correct or
incorrect without checking it.

### An export option is not visible

The option may not be supported in the current version, may be located
elsewhere, or may only be available for certain report types. Check the
current interface and version-specific documentation. Do not assume an
unavailable control is hidden or that the feature is guaranteed.

### A report does not match a bank or cash balance

A report may include only selected transactions or a selected period.
Opening balances, transfers, debt, pending payments, unrecorded
activity, and other factors can cause the figures to differ. Compare
like-for-like information and investigate the difference rather than
forcing the numbers to match.

## 12.13 Record Accuracy and Reconciliation

A report summarizes the recorded information; it does not independently
confirm that the records are complete.

When accuracy is important, compare the report with appropriate source
documents, such as receipts, invoices, payment confirmations, bank
statements, or other records relevant to the activity.

Differences may occur because:

-   A transaction was not entered.
-   A transaction was entered more than once.
-   The date or amount was entered incorrectly.
-   A transaction was assigned to the wrong category.
-   The report uses a different date range.
-   The report includes different types of transactions.
-   A transfer, loan repayment, or allocation is treated differently
    from an ordinary income or expense entry.

### Correcting a discrepancy

1.  Identify the exact figure that differs.
2.  Confirm the period and transaction types being compared.
3.  Find the relevant underlying entries.
4.  Compare those entries with the appropriate source records.
5.  Correct an entry only when you know what was recorded incorrectly
    and the application supports the correction.
6.  Reopen or regenerate the report, if supported, and review the
    result.

Do not change original records solely to force a report to match an
external figure. For formal accounting, taxation, lending, or business
compliance, confirm the records and documents required for your
particular purpose.

## 12.14 Backups and Exported Reports Are Different

An exported report is usually intended for viewing, printing, sharing,
or analysis. It may contain only a summary or a subset of the
application's information.

A backup is intended to preserve data for recovery, subject to the
application's actual backup design. A PDF or CSV should not be treated
as a complete backup unless the application explicitly documents that
capability.

Before clearing application storage, changing devices, or reinstalling
the application:

-   Read the separate backup and restoration instructions.
-   Confirm what the backup contains.
-   Follow the documented backup process.
-   Do not delete the original data until you have verified the backup
    according to the available workflow.

Keeping a report file can be useful for reference, but it is not a
substitute for a supported backup and restore process.

## 12.15 Practical Monthly Review Example

Suppose a user reviews the following recorded activity for one month:

  Item                             Amount
  ------------------------- -------------
  Total recorded income           ₹32,000
  Total recorded expenses         ₹21,500
  **Difference**              **₹10,500**

The calculation is:

`₹32,000 - ₹21,500 = ₹10,500`

A careful monthly review could follow this sequence:

1.  Confirm that the report covers the intended month.
2.  Review the recorded income entries.
3.  Review the recorded expense entries.
4.  Check category totals for inconsistent categorization.
5.  Look for missing or duplicate transactions.
6.  Compare important entries with receipts or payment records where
    needed.
7.  Save or print a report copy if the relevant export feature is
    supported.
8.  Store the file securely and keep a separate supported backup of
    application data.

The ₹10,500 difference is only the result of subtracting the recorded
expenses from recorded income. It may not equal the money currently
available in cash or a bank account because transfers, prior balances,
debts, pending payments, and unrecorded transactions may not be
included.

This example demonstrates how a report can support a review; it does not
establish a complete financial position without additional information.

Before relying on or sharing a report, use this checklist:

-   [ ] The correct report type is selected.
-   [ ] The date range is correct.
-   [ ] Currency and number formats are clear.
-   [ ] Income and expense totals have been reviewed.
-   [ ] Categories and transactions are assigned consistently.
-   [ ] Missing or duplicate entries have been considered.
-   [ ] Charts and tables use the intended filters.
-   [ ] The meaning of any difference or balance is understood.
-   [ ] The exported file opens correctly, if applicable.
-   [ ] All pages and values are readable.
-   [ ] Sensitive information has been reviewed before sharing.
-   [ ] Important source records are maintained separately.
-   [ ] A supported backup is maintained separately from report exports.

## 12.17 Important Notice

Reports, charts, PDF statements, and spreadsheet exports are intended to
support financial organization and review. They should not automatically
be treated as audited accounts, official bank statements, tax filings,
or professional accounting advice.

The accuracy of a report depends on the information entered, the filters
applied, and the application's calculation rules. Verify important
figures against appropriate source records before using them for formal,
business, tax, lending, or other financial purposes.

Feature availability and interface details may differ between versions
of Daily Khata Pro. Use only the reporting, chart, print, and export
controls that are actually available in your installed version.
