## 1.1 Welcome to Daily Khata Pro

Welcome to the official user documentation for **Daily Khata Pro**.

Daily Khata Pro is designed to help users maintain organized financial
records and access practical tools for everyday money management.
Depending on the features available in the installed version, users may
be able to record income and expenses, review records, organize
financial goals, use calculators, prepare supported reports, create
invoices, and maintain other financial or work-related records.

This guide explains the app's general purpose, how to approach its main
areas, how to use records responsibly, and what limitations users should
understand before relying on the information it produces.

Daily Khata Pro is a record-keeping and calculation utility. It does not
independently verify every entry, guarantee a financial outcome, or
replace a qualified accountant, tax professional, lawyer, financial
adviser, bank, or other regulated service.

> **Version note:** App screens, labels, workflows, and feature
> availability may change between versions. If an option described here
> is not present in your installed version, use the controls that are
> actually available in that version and consult the relevant chapter.

## 1.2 What Is Daily Khata Pro?

Daily Khata Pro brings supported financial-recording and calculation
tools into one interface. It can be useful for people who want to record
financial activity, review earlier entries, calculate estimates, and
keep related information organized.

Depending on the installed version, the app may provide tools or
sections for:

-   Income and expense records.
-   Transaction history and record review.
-   Financial goals and milestones.
-   Smart Fund Allocation.
-   Calculators and financial planning utilities.
-   Reports, charts, summaries, and printable records.
-   GST and non-GST invoice generation.
-   Loans, EMIs, and other financial records.
-   Split bills and group expenses.
-   Work attendance and shift-related records.
-   Personal notes or private information tools.
-   Backup, export, import, or Google Drive-related functions.
-   Other utilities described in the relevant chapters.

The list above describes areas covered by the documentation; it is not a
promise that every feature is available in every release. Check your
current app version before relying on a specific tool.

### What the app is intended to help with

The general purpose is to help users **record, organize, review,
calculate, and plan**.

For example, a freelancer might record a client payment, enter a
work-related expense, review the resulting records, and use a calculator
to estimate a future payment. A household might record regular income
and expenses and review a summary. A small business user might prepare
an invoice using the invoice tool, if available.

These examples illustrate possible uses. Users should select the
features that match their own needs and the actual functionality
available in their app.

## 1.3 Who May Find the App Useful?

### Individuals

Individuals can use supported record-keeping features to organize
personal income, expenses, payments, and financial goals.

### Freelancers and independent workers

Freelancers may use the app to record payments received from clients,
note expenses, and maintain a personal reference of work-related
financial activity. The app should not be treated as a replacement for
any accounting records or tax documents legally required for a business.

### Small business owners and shopkeepers

Business users may use supported tools to organize day-to-day records,
review payment information, and prepare invoices where invoice
functionality is available. Business owners remain responsible for
checking entries, tax treatment, invoice details, and applicable legal
requirements.

### Households and families

Households may use financial records to understand routine income and
expenses and to organize shared financial information. Users should take
care when sharing devices, reports, or exported files that contain
private information.

### Workers and contractors

Where attendance or shift-related features are available, workers may
record workdays, attendance statuses, hours, wages, advances, or pending
payments according to the fields supported by the app. Records should be
checked against employer or contractor records before being used to
settle a dispute or make a formal claim.

## 1.4 The Main Workflow: Record → Organize → Review → Plan

Daily Khata Pro is easiest to understand as a four-part workflow.

### 1. Record

Enter information into the appropriate available section. Examples
include an income entry, an expense, a financial goal, or another
supported record.

Before saving, check the amount, date, category, description, and any
other important fields. Use clear descriptions that will still make
sense when you review the entry later.

### 2. Organize

Use available categories, record types, filters, dates, or other
controls to keep information understandable. Consistent naming and
categorization make later reviews more useful.

### 3. Review

Review saved records, balances, calculations, reports, or summaries. Do
not assume that a total is correct simply because the app displays it:
totals are based on the data and formulas available to the app.

### 4. Plan

Use available goals, allocation tools, and calculators to explore
possible scenarios. Treat estimates as planning information, not a
guarantee of future income, savings, returns, or other outcomes.

## 1.5 Main Navigation and Common Areas

The current interface may provide navigation areas such as **Home,
Records, Add, Goals, Calculator, and Reports**. The exact labels and
layout can vary by version or screen size.

### Home

The Home area is the main dashboard. It may display high-level financial
information, summaries, balances, or shortcuts to common tasks.

**How to use it:** 1. Open Home. 2. Review the displayed summary cards
and information. 3. If a figure looks unexpected, open the relevant
records or report to investigate it. 4. Do not treat a dashboard summary
as a substitute for reviewing individual transactions.

**Example:** If a balance looks different from what you expected, check
whether an income or expense was entered twice, entered with the wrong
amount, or assigned to an unintended record type.

### Records

Records is used to review saved information and, where supported, manage
existing entries.

**How to use it:** 1. Open Records. 2. Find the relevant record or use
available search, filter, or category controls. 3. Open or review the
entry. 4. Correct an error using the edit controls available in your
version. 5. Confirm the resulting summary or report if the change
affects totals.

Avoid deleting records unless you understand the effect and have
retained any information you still need.

### Add

Add provides access to available options for creating new records or
opening additional tools. The central Add button may expose more than
one record type.

**How to use it:** 1. Open Add. 2. Select the type of record or tool you
need. 3. Complete the required fields. 4. Review the entered
information. 5. Save or confirm using the control shown by the app. 6.
Reopen Records or the relevant section to verify the result when
appropriate.

If you cannot find a particular option, it may be named differently,
located in another section, or unavailable in your installed version.

### Goals

Goals is intended for supported financial goal and milestone features.

**How to use it:** 1. Open Goals. 2. Create or select a goal if the
feature is available. 3. Enter the requested target information. 4.
Review progress using the information shown by the app. 5. Update the
goal when your circumstances or plans change.

A goal is a planning target. Creating one does not automatically reserve
money, move funds between bank accounts, or guarantee that the target
will be reached unless a separate, explicitly supported function says
otherwise.

### Calculator

Calculator provides access to supported mathematical and financial
tools. Depending on the version, the menu may include standard
arithmetic, currency conversion, loan EMI, SIP or wealth estimates, fund
allocation, GST, discounts or margins, goals or inflation, and other
calculation modes.

**How to use it:** 1. Open Calculator. 2. Select the required calculator
mode. 3. Read the field labels and enter the requested inputs. 4. Check
units, dates, percentages, and currency. 5. Review the result and any
assumptions or breakdown shown. 6. Verify important figures
independently before using them in a transaction, contract, tax filing,
loan decision, or other formal purpose.

Different calculators use different inputs and assumptions. Refer to the
dedicated calculator chapter for the relevant mode.

### Reports

Reports provides access to supported summaries, charts, statements, or
export and print options.

**How to use it:** 1. Open Reports. 2. Select the report type or period,
if the app provides those controls. 3. Review the records included in
the report. 4. Check the dates, categories, totals, and any displayed
filters. 5. Preview the report before printing or exporting, where
supported. 6. Store exported files carefully because they may contain
sensitive financial information.

An exported report is a representation of the app's available data. It
does not independently confirm that the underlying transactions are
genuine or complete.

## 1.6 Three Important Areas of the App

### A. Financial records

The record-keeping areas help users keep supported financial information
organized. Records may include income, expenses, goals, work-related
information, and other entries depending on the installed version.

Good records should be accurate, understandable, and consistent. Use
meaningful descriptions, check dates, and avoid duplicate entries.

### B. Planning and allocation tools

Planning features, including Smart Fund Allocation where available, help
users organize how they intend to distribute or plan money. These
features are tools for organizing information and exploring scenarios.

They do not automatically transfer money, create a bank account, make an
investment, or guarantee a particular result unless the app explicitly
provides and documents such an action.

If a percentage-based allocation screen requires a total of 100%, verify
the configured percentages before relying on the displayed breakdown.
For example, allocations of 50%, 30%, and 20% total 100%. Allocations of
50%, 30%, and 10% total only 90%, leaving 10% unallocated under that
example.

### C. Reports, invoices, and printable records

Where supported, reporting and invoice tools can format information for
review, printing, or sharing. Users should check every important field
before using a generated document for business, tax, reimbursement, or
legal purposes.

For invoices, check the seller and buyer details, invoice number, dates,
item descriptions, quantities, rates, discounts, tax settings, totals,
payment information, and terms. The correct tax treatment depends on the
transaction and applicable rules; do not rely on a software label alone
to determine legal compliance.

## 1.7 A Practical Example: Recording a Routine Expense

This example explains the general process without assuming that every
screen uses identical labels.

**Situation:** You pay ₹250 for office supplies.

1.  Open Add or the expense-entry area available in your version.
2.  Choose the expense record type, if prompted.
3.  Enter `250` as the amount.
4.  Select the appropriate date.
5.  Choose a relevant category if categories are available.
6.  Add a short description such as `Office supplies`.
7.  Review the entry for typing errors.
8.  Save the record.
9.  Open Records or the relevant summary to confirm that the entry
    appears as expected.

If the entry does not appear, do not immediately add it again. First
check whether the original entry was saved, whether a filter is hiding
it, and whether the app displayed an error.

## 1.8 A Practical Example: Reviewing a Financial Summary

**Situation:** You want to understand why the displayed total differs
from your own calculation.

1.  Open Home or Reports.
2.  Identify the figure that seems incorrect.
3.  Open the relevant record list.
4.  Check the date range, category, and record type used by the summary,
    where those controls are available.
5.  Look for missing, duplicate, or incorrectly entered records.
6.  Correct any confirmed errors using the app's available controls.
7.  Recheck the summary.

Remember that a summary can be mathematically consistent with the data
stored in the app while still being different from your real-world
finances if some entries are missing or incorrect.

## 1.9 Offline Use and Internet-Dependent Features

Daily Khata Pro is described as supporting offline use for functionality
implemented locally in the app. Whether a particular action works
offline depends on how that feature is implemented.

### Local operations

A feature that relies only on locally available code and data may
continue to work without an internet connection.

### Internet-dependent operations

Functions that communicate with an external service generally need a
working connection. Examples may include Google Drive authorization or
synchronization, external data services, online resources, or
third-party integrations.

**Do not assume that every feature is offline-capable.** If a task fails
while offline, reconnect when appropriate and check the relevant
feature's documentation.

## 1.10 Local Storage, Privacy, and Data Control

Some Daily Khata Pro functionality is designed to work with data stored
in the local browser or device environment. The exact storage model and
data flow depend on the app's implementation and version.

Local storage can reduce reliance on a remote application database for
supported records, but **local storage is not the same as a backup** and
should not be treated as a guarantee that information can never be lost
or accessed by another person.

Local records may be affected by: - Clearing browser or site data. -
Resetting application data. - Removing or reinstalling the app,
depending on how it is installed and stores data. - Changing browsers or
devices. - Device loss, damage, or storage problems. - Accidental
deletion or other system actions.

Users should protect their device, use available access controls, and
understand the backup and restore process before resetting storage or
changing devices.

### Privacy does not mean zero risk

No software can responsibly guarantee absolute privacy or permanent data
availability in every situation. Avoid sharing private financial records
unnecessarily, and handle exported files, screenshots, and backup copies
carefully.

## 1.11 Backups and Moving to Another Device

If the app provides backup, export, import, restore, or Google
Drive-related features, review the dedicated backup chapter before
changing devices or clearing storage.

A careful general workflow is:

1.  Open the available backup or export feature.
2.  Follow the options shown by the installed version.
3.  Wait for the operation to finish and check any success or error
    message.
4.  Confirm that the backup file or destination exists when the app
    provides a way to verify it.
5.  Store a copy in a safe location.
6.  Before restoring, understand whether the operation merges, replaces,
    or overwrites existing records.
7.  After restoring, review important records and totals.

Do not assume that a backup contains every type of application data
unless the app's documentation or interface confirms it. Do not delete
the original data until you have verified that the backup is usable and
that you understand the restore process.

## 1.12 App Lock and Security Controls

If your installed version provides a passcode or PIN lock, use it as one
layer of access protection.

Recommended practices: - Choose a PIN that is not easy to guess. - Do
not share it unnecessarily. - Keep recovery information private. -
Understand the available recovery process before relying on the lock. -
Use your device's screen lock as well. - Keep exported files and backups
protected.

An app lock is not automatically proof that every stored file or
database is encrypted. The security chapter explains the available
controls and their limitations. Use only the protections actually
implemented in your version.

## 1.13 Financial Calculations: Inputs, Assumptions, and Verification

Calculators provide results based on the inputs, formulas, settings, and
assumptions used by the app. A result can be incorrect for your purpose
if an input is wrong, a rate is outdated, or a real-world fee or
condition is not included in the calculation.

Before relying on a calculation, check: - The amount entered. - The
currency and unit. - The interest rate or percentage format. - The time
period or tenure. - The selected calculation mode. - Any fees, taxes,
spreads, or assumptions displayed. - Whether the result is an estimate
or a final confirmed amount.

For example, a currency conversion result based on one exchange rate may
differ from the amount actually received from a bank or payment provider
because of rate changes, fees, and provider-specific terms.

For loans, tax calculations, investments, and other important decisions,
compare the result with the relevant official documents or provider
information.

## 1.14 Data Accuracy and User Responsibility

The app can process information entered into it, but it cannot
independently confirm every real-world transaction.

Users should check: - Amounts and decimal places. - Dates and time
periods. - Income or expense type. - Categories and descriptions. -
Payment status and related details. - Allocation percentages. - Imported
or restored information. - Invoice details and tax settings. - Report
filters and totals.

**Example:** If an expense of ₹500 is entered as ₹5,000, the app may
calculate a summary correctly using the entered ₹5,000. The error is in
the input, not necessarily in the arithmetic.

For important records, compare the app's information with receipts,
invoices, statements, agreements, or other original sources.

## 1.15 What Daily Khata Pro Does Not Guarantee

Daily Khata Pro does not, by itself, guarantee: - Financial profit or
investment returns. - Achievement of a savings or financial goal. - That
user-entered records are complete or accurate. - Recovery of deleted,
damaged, or inaccessible local data. - That every feature is available
in every version. - That external services will always be available. -
That a generated report or invoice automatically satisfies every legal
or tax requirement. - That an estimate matches the final amount charged,
paid, received, or approved by a third party.

Users remain responsible for their records, decisions, backups, and
verification of important information.
