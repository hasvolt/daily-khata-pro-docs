# Chapter 11: Category Budgets & Spending Limits

## 11.1 Overview

The **Category Budgets & Spending Limits** feature helps users plan how
much they intend to spend in different areas and compare recorded
expenses against those limits. Instead of reviewing all spending as one
total, users can organize expenses into categories such as groceries,
transport, household bills, dining, entertainment, health, work-related
costs, or other personal and business needs.

A budget is a planning reference, not a restriction imposed by the
application. Its usefulness depends on choosing realistic amounts,
recording expenses consistently, and reviewing the results regularly.

The exact controls, progress indicators, alerts, date ranges, and
calculations described in this chapter depend on the features
implemented in the installed version of Daily Khata Pro. If a control is
not visible in your version, do not assume it is available.

## 11.2 What Is a Category Budget?

A **category budget** is an amount planned for a particular type of
expense over a chosen period, such as a week or month, if the
application supports that period.

For example, a user might plan separate limits for:

-   Groceries and household supplies.
-   Electricity, phone, internet, and other bills.
-   Transport and fuel.
-   Dining and entertainment.
-   Health and personal care.
-   Work-related expenses.
-   Home maintenance.
-   Other planned spending.

Separate categories make it easier to understand where money is being
used. Choose category names that reflect your actual needs and are clear
enough to avoid confusion.

A budget is not necessarily money physically separated into a different
bank account or wallet. Unless the application explicitly performs and
confirms a real transfer, a budget should be treated as a planning
record.

## 11.3 What Is a Spending Limit?

A **spending limit** is the maximum amount a user intends to spend in a
category during a defined period.

For example, if the planned monthly dining limit is ₹3,000, you can
compare recorded dining expenses with that amount.

A limit is a planning reference. It does not automatically prevent a
payment, block a purchase, or guarantee that spending will remain within
the planned amount.

## 11.4 Why Category Budgets Are Useful

Category budgets can help users:

-   Plan expected expenses before a budget period begins.
-   Identify categories where spending is increasing.
-   Compare planned spending with recorded expenses.
-   Notice when a category is approaching or exceeding its limit, if the
    application shows this information.
-   Review recurring household or business costs.
-   Identify missing or incorrectly categorized entries.
-   Make more informed adjustments to future budgets.

Budgets are most useful when based on realistic needs rather than
estimates that are too strict to maintain. They should help you
understand your spending, not encourage you to ignore essential needs or
hide genuine expenses.

## 11.5 Understanding Budget Amounts

A basic category budget usually involves three values.

**Budget limit:** The amount planned for the category during the
selected period.

**Spent amount:** The total of expenses recorded against that category
during the relevant period, according to the application's calculation
rules.

**Remaining amount:** The difference between the budget limit and
recorded expenses.

The basic calculation is:

`Remaining Amount = Budget Limit − Recorded Expenses`

### Example

Suppose the monthly grocery budget is ₹5,000 and recorded grocery
expenses total ₹3,200.

  Item                  Amount
  ------------------- --------
  Budget limit          ₹5,000
  Recorded expenses     ₹3,200
  Remaining amount      ₹1,800

Calculation:

`₹5,000 − ₹3,200 = ₹1,800`

If recorded expenses become greater than the budget limit, the remaining
amount becomes negative under this formula. This indicates that recorded
spending has exceeded the planned limit.

These figures are only as accurate as the transactions, dates, and
categories entered by the user. The application may display the result
differently depending on its implementation.

## 11.6 Setting Up a Category Budget

If your installed version includes category budget settings, use the
following general workflow.

### Step 1: Identify the category

Choose the expense category for which you want to set a limit. Use an
existing category or create one only if the application supports custom
categories.

Examples include groceries, transport, household bills, dining, or
work-related expenses.

### Step 2: Choose the budget period

Select the applicable period, such as monthly, if the application
provides that option. Use the same period when comparing the limit with
expenses.

Do not assume that a budget automatically resets at the beginning of a
new month. Check how your installed version handles periods and existing
budgets.

### Step 3: Enter a planned amount

Enter the amount you reasonably expect to spend in that category during
the selected period. Check the currency and number format before saving,
and avoid extra zeros or unintended decimal places.

### Step 4: Save or confirm the budget

Save or confirm the entry if a control is available. Reopen the relevant
budget view to verify that the amount was stored correctly.

### Step 5: Review recorded expenses

As expenses are recorded, review the category total and compare it with
the planned limit. Confirm that transactions have been assigned to the
intended categories and that the selected period is correct.

The exact fields and workflow may differ by application version. Use
only the controls actually available.

## 11.7 Understanding Progress Indicators

Some versions of a budgeting interface may display a progress bar or
percentage showing how much of a budget has been used. If available,
these indicators can provide a quick visual summary.

A typical percentage calculation is:

`Budget Used (%) = (Recorded Expenses ÷ Budget Limit) × 100`

### Example

If the budget limit is ₹4,000 and recorded expenses total ₹2,000:

`(₹2,000 ÷ ₹4,000) × 100 = 50%`

This means that recorded expenses equal 50% of the planned limit.

Interpret a progress indicator carefully:

-   A low percentage does not necessarily mean all upcoming expenses are
    covered.
-   A high percentage may be expected near the end of the budget period.
-   An amount over 100% means recorded expenses exceed the limit under
    this formula.
-   A zero or unexpected percentage may result from missing entries,
    incorrect categories, an incorrect period, or an improperly
    configured limit.
-   If the budget limit is zero, a percentage calculation cannot be
    performed using the formula above.

This formula is explanatory. The exact display and calculation method
depend on the application's implementation.

## 11.8 Understanding the Remaining Budget

The remaining amount helps show how much of the planned budget is left
according to recorded expenses.

### Example

  Item                            Amount
  ----------------------------- --------
  Monthly transport budget        ₹2,500
  Recorded transport expenses     ₹1,750
  Remaining budget                  ₹750

Calculation:

`₹2,500 − ₹1,750 = ₹750`

If expenses exceed the limit, the remaining amount may be negative. Do
not treat a negative amount as money available to spend; it indicates
that the planned limit has been exceeded.

Also remember that a displayed remaining amount does not necessarily
equal your actual bank balance or cash available. It is a calculation
based on the budget and records represented in the application.

If the application uses a different display convention, follow the value
and explanation shown by the installed version.

## 11.9 Creating Realistic Limits

A useful budget should be practical and based on actual circumstances.

Consider these practices:

-   Review previous expenses where records are available.
-   Include regular bills and necessary household costs.
-   Account for expenses that occur only occasionally.
-   Avoid setting limits so low that they cannot reasonably cover
    essential needs.
-   Review the budget when income, prices, responsibilities, or work
    requirements change.
-   Keep planned savings separate from expense limits where the
    application supports that distinction.
-   Leave room for reasonable uncertainty where costs vary.
-   Distinguish necessary expenses from optional purchases when that
    helps your planning.

A budget is a planning tool. Adjust it when circumstances change rather
than treating it as a promise that cannot be revised.

## 11.10 Reviewing Spending During the Budget Period

Do not wait until the end of the month to review every category.
Periodic reviews can help identify missing entries and unexpected
increases.

A simple review routine is:

1.  Open the relevant budget or expense view, if available.
2.  Check the recorded total for each category.
3.  Confirm that recent expenses have been entered.
4.  Correct duplicate or incorrectly categorized transactions, if
    editing is supported.
5.  Compare recorded expenses with the planned limit.
6.  Review categories that are approaching or have exceeded their
    limits.
7.  Adjust future plans when necessary.

The frequency of review is a personal choice. Weekly reviews may be
useful for categories with frequent transactions, while less active
categories may need fewer checks.

## 11.11 What to Do When a Limit Is Reached

Reaching a budget limit is a signal to review the situation, not proof
that a mistake has occurred.

When a category reaches or exceeds its limit:

-   Verify that the recorded transactions are accurate.
-   Check whether an expense was assigned to the wrong category.
-   Confirm that the expense belongs to the selected budget period.
-   Determine whether the expense was essential, unexpected, or
    discretionary.
-   Review whether the original limit was realistic.
-   Consider adjusting future budgets if underlying costs have changed.

Do not omit genuine expenses merely to make a category appear within its
limit. Accurate records are more useful than figures that look better
but do not reflect actual spending.

If an unexpected essential expense causes an overrun, record it
accurately and review what it means for the rest of the budget. The
application does not decide which expenses are necessary; that decision
depends on your circumstances.

## 11.12 Budget Changes and New Periods

When a new budget period begins, verify how your version handles
existing limits and recorded transactions. Some implementations may
carry settings forward, while others may require the user to configure a
new period.

Before changing or resetting a budget:

-   Confirm the period to which the budget applies.
-   Check whether previous records will remain available.
-   Avoid deleting transaction history simply to start a new budget
    period.
-   Review any available instructions for editing or resetting budgets.
-   Confirm the result after saving changes.

Do not assume that changing a budget limit changes the underlying
transaction records. A budget adjustment and an expense correction are
different actions.

## 11.13 Category Consistency and Transaction Accuracy

Category budgets depend on expenses being assigned consistently.

For example, if fuel expenses are sometimes recorded under **Transport**
and sometimes under **Other**, the Transport total may not show the full
amount spent on travel.

To keep summaries useful:

-   Use the same category for similar expenses where practical.
-   Review category names before saving a transaction.
-   Avoid creating multiple categories with nearly identical meanings
    unless there is a clear reason.
-   Correct miscategorized transactions if editing is supported.
-   Check for duplicate entries.
-   Confirm that dates match the intended budget period.

If you change the category or amount of a transaction, the category
totals may change as a result. Review the updated totals after editing.

## 11.14 Practical Example: Reviewing a Monthly Budget

Suppose a household sets the following monthly limits:

  Category            Planned limit   Recorded expenses   Remaining amount
  ----------------- --------------- ------------------- ------------------
  Groceries                  ₹6,000              ₹4,500             ₹1,500
  Transport                  ₹2,000              ₹1,600               ₹400
  Dining                     ₹2,500              ₹2,700              -₹200
  Household bills            ₹3,500              ₹3,200               ₹300
  **Total**             **₹14,000**         **₹12,000**         **₹2,000**

In this example, groceries, transport, and household bills remain below
their planned limits. Dining has exceeded its limit by ₹200.

The next step is to check whether the dining entries are accurate and
consider whether the limit should be adjusted for a future period. The
figures do not by themselves determine whether the spending was
appropriate; the user's needs and circumstances matter.

The total remaining amount should also be interpreted carefully. A
positive combined figure does not mean that one category's unused budget
automatically offsets another category's overspending. Category-level
limits and total spending answer different planning questions.

## 11.15 Common Problems and Troubleshooting

### The spent amount looks incorrect

Check whether all relevant expenses have been recorded and assigned to
the correct category. Look for duplicate entries and verify the date or
budget period.

### The remaining amount does not match expectations

Recalculate the difference between the budget limit and recorded
expenses. Confirm that both values refer to the same period and
category.

### A transaction appears in the wrong category

If editing is supported, correct the category on the transaction record.
Then review the affected category totals again.

### A budget or progress indicator is not visible

The control may not be available in your current version, may be located
elsewhere, or may require a budget to be configured first. Check the
current interface and version-specific documentation.

### The displayed total changes after editing a transaction

This may occur when an expense amount, date, or category is changed.
Review the updated transaction and affected category total to confirm
that the result is expected.

### The budget seems to include expenses from another period

Check the date on each transaction and the budget period selected. If
the application has specific date-range rules, follow the behavior shown
by the installed version.

### A budget was changed but previous records also appear different

Review whether you changed only the limit or also edited transaction
records. Do not assume that changing a budget automatically modifies or
deletes previous transactions.

## 11.16 Data Accuracy and Privacy

Budget summaries are calculated from the financial information available
to the application. Incorrect, incomplete, or duplicated entries can
make the summary misleading.

For reliable records:

-   Enter amounts carefully.
-   Use consistent categories.
-   Correct mistakes promptly.
-   Keep independent backups of important financial records.
-   Protect access to your device and any exported files.
-   Review records before sharing screenshots, exports, or reports.
-   Avoid including unnecessary sensitive details in transaction notes.

If the application stores financial data locally, removing browser or
application data may affect saved information. Follow the application's
backup and restoration instructions before clearing storage or changing
devices.

## 11.17 Recommended Budget Review Checklist

Use this checklist when reviewing a budget:

-   [ ] The correct budget period is selected.
-   [ ] Each budget has a clear category and amount.
-   [ ] Recent expenses have been recorded.
-   [ ] Transactions are assigned to the correct categories.
-   [ ] Duplicate or incorrect entries have been checked.
-   [ ] Remaining amounts have been reviewed.
-   [ ] Categories that exceeded their limits have been examined.
-   [ ] Budget changes reflect current needs and costs.
-   [ ] Important financial records are backed up appropriately.

## 11.18 Important Notice

Category budgets and spending limits are intended for personal planning
and record-keeping. They do not automatically block transactions,
guarantee savings, or provide professional financial advice unless a
specific implemented feature clearly states otherwise.

The accuracy of budget summaries depends on the information entered and
the application's calculation rules. Verify important figures before
relying on them for formal accounting, business, tax, or other financial
purposes.
