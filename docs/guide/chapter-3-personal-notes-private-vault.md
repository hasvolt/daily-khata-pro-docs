# Chapter 3: Personal Notes & Private Vault

## Overview

The **Personal Notes & Private Vault** section of Daily Khata Pro is
intended to provide a separate workspace for general written information
that does not belong in routine income, expense, balance, or transaction
records.

Depending on the features available in the installed version, users may
use this area for business ideas, personal planning, meeting notes,
research notes, reminders, project observations, and other text-based
information.

This workspace is separate from the financial-record workflow. Writing a
note does **not** automatically create an income entry, expense entry,
payment record, reminder notification, or accounting transaction.

> **Version note:** Available controls may vary by app version. Note
> creation, editing, masking, categories, tags, search, export, and
> backup should only be used when those options are present in the
> installed version.

## 3.1 Purpose of the Personal Notes Vault

Financial records and general notes have different purposes:

-   **Financial records** document actual money movements, transactions,
    and balances.
-   **Personal notes** store context, ideas, observations, plans, and
    information that may be useful later.

The Personal Notes Vault is intended to help users:

-   Keep general notes in one location.
-   Separate written information from income, expenses, and balances.
-   Record ideas and plans for later review.
-   Organize notes with available categories, tags, colors, or labels.
-   Find information through search or filtering when supported.
-   Conceal note contents in the normal interface if an individual-note
    masking option is available.
-   Export or back up notes if the installed version supports those
    functions.

Use the vault for information that is appropriate to store on your
device. Consider the sensitivity of the information before saving it.

## 3.2 Examples of Information You Can Record

The following are examples of how a notes workspace may be used. They
are suggested use cases, not guaranteed built-in templates.

### Business and Work Ideas

Record ideas for a business, service, project, product, or future
improvement.

Useful details may include: - The idea or problem you want to address. -
Why the idea matters. - Questions that still require research. - The
next action to consider. - A date or project name for future reference.

**Example**

**Title:** Electrical Service Process --- Improvement Ideas

**Note:** Review the service-booking process, list common customer
questions, and identify which details should be included in the
technician's service checklist.

**Next action:** Review the list before changing the live workflow.

### Personal Planning

Keep non-financial planning notes, routine reminders, task lists, and
points you want to review later.

**Example**

**Title:** Weekly Planning

**Note:** Review unfinished tasks, organize work priorities, and prepare
a list of items that need attention during the week.

A saved note should not be assumed to trigger an alarm or push
notification unless the application explicitly provides that feature and
it has been configured.

### Meeting and Discussion Notes

Record the main points from a meeting or discussion, decisions made,
follow-up tasks, and questions that still need answers.

Avoid recording another person's confidential information unless you
have a valid reason and permission to retain it.

### Research Notes

Store short summaries, observations, questions, and references from
research.

For important claims, keep the original source or reference so that you
can check the information later. A note containing a claim is not, by
itself, proof that the claim is accurate.

### Financial Planning Notes

You may use notes to outline a budget, savings idea, upcoming expense,
or financial decision.

However, actual income, expenses, payments, and balances should be
recorded in the appropriate financial section when that functionality is
available. A note about a planned purchase is not the same as a recorded
expense.

### Project and Follow-up Notes

Use notes to remember project details, unresolved questions, or tasks
that need another review. If the app does not provide task management or
notification features for notes, do not rely on a note as an automatic
reminder.

## 3.3 Understanding the Private Workspace

The vault is intended to be a separate workspace for written
information, not a replacement for the app's financial records.

Keeping notes separate can make financial records easier to understand
and reduce the risk of mixing narrative information with transaction
data.

For example:

-   A plan to buy equipment belongs in a note.
-   After the purchase actually occurs, record the expense in the
    relevant financial section if appropriate.
-   A discussion about a payment can be recorded as a note.
-   The actual received payment should be recorded in the relevant
    financial section.

The word **private** describes the intended purpose of this workspace.
It does not guarantee protection against every form of device access,
data loss, malware, unauthorized use, or access to browser storage and
backup files.

## 3.4 Creating a Clear and Useful Note

If the installed version provides a note-creation option, use a
consistent structure so that information remains understandable later.

A practical note structure is:

1.  **Title:** Use a short, descriptive name.
2.  **Date or context:** Add a date or project name when it helps
    explain the note.
3.  **Details:** Write the information in clear, specific language.
4.  **Next action:** Record what needs to happen next, if anything.
5.  **Category or tag:** Use an available label if the app supports it.

### Example Note

**Title:** Website Documentation --- Pending Improvements

**Date or context:** Documentation review

**Details:** Review the current user guide, identify sections that need
clearer explanations, and check that feature descriptions match the
installed app.

**Next action:** Verify the relevant screens before publishing changes.

This is an example of good note organization, not a guaranteed built-in
template.

## 3.5 Organizing Notes with Colors, Categories, or Tags

The interface may provide color labels, categories, tags, or search
filters. Use only the controls that are actually available in your
version.

If color labels are supported, give each color a consistent meaning. For
example, one color could represent work-related notes and another could
represent personal planning. Do not rely on color alone if a clear title
or written category would make the note easier to identify.

If categories or tags are available:

-   Use short, consistent names.
-   Avoid creating many nearly identical labels.
-   Choose labels that describe the subject.
-   Check existing labels before creating duplicates.
-   Remember that tags are organizational aids, not security boundaries.

If search is available, use distinctive words in the note title and
content. Avoid putting sensitive details in titles if titles may appear
in a list or preview.

## 3.6 Individual Note Masking or Concealment

The application interface describes an option for masking individual
notes. When this option is available, it may conceal some or all of a
note's contents in the normal viewing interface until the user reveals
them.

Use the control according to the instructions shown in the app. Check
what is actually concealed: the entire note, its body text, its title,
or only its preview.

**Important:** Hiding or masking information on screen is not
necessarily the same as encrypting stored data. Do not assume that a
masked note is protected from someone who can access the device, browser
storage, application files, or exported backups.

For sensitive information:

-   Keep the device protected with its own screen lock.
-   Avoid sharing an unlocked device or application unnecessarily.
-   Do not store passwords, one-time codes, payment-card security codes,
    or other authentication secrets in ordinary notes.
-   Store only sensitive information that you genuinely need.
-   Review the app's privacy and backup behavior before relying on it
    for confidential records.

## 3.8 Searching and Reviewing Notes

If search or filtering is available, use it to locate notes by title,
keyword, category, or tag, depending on the supported controls.

For easier retrieval:

-   Give each note a meaningful title.
-   Put the main subject near the beginning.
-   Use consistent names for recurring projects.
-   Add dates when timing matters.
-   Review or archive outdated information if the app provides a
    suitable option.

Search results depend on the available search functionality and the
words stored in a note. If a note cannot be found, check spelling and
try a shorter, distinctive keyword. Do not assume that the app searches
every field unless its behavior confirms that.

## 3.9 Editing, Saving, and Deleting Notes

Review notes periodically so that old information does not become
misleading.

When editing a note:

1.  Open the intended note.
2.  Confirm that it is the correct item.
3.  Make the necessary changes.
4.  Save or confirm the change using the available control.
5.  Reopen the note, if needed, to verify that the updated content was
    retained.

If the app provides a delete option, use it carefully. Before deleting a
note, consider whether the information may be needed later and whether a
backup contains a copy. Do not assume that a deleted note can always be
recovered.

If a change does not appear to have been saved, avoid repeatedly editing
the same note until you have checked whether the previous change was
retained.

## 3.10 Exporting and Backing Up Notes

The application interface indicates that note data may be included in an
export or backup workflow, including an encrypted JSON backup option.
The exact data coverage, encryption behavior, and restoration process
should be confirmed from the relevant backup feature and documentation
in the installed version.

A backup is useful only if it can be located and restored when needed.

Recommended practices:

-   Create backups regularly when the feature is available.
-   Save backup files in a location you control.
-   Consider keeping another copy in a separate, secure location.
-   Do not share backup files publicly; they may contain private
    information.
-   Verify that an export completed successfully.
-   Follow the dedicated backup and restoration documentation before
    changing devices or clearing app data.
-   Do not assume that exporting a file removes the original note from
    the app.

If a backup is described as encrypted, protect the backup file and any
required recovery information. Encryption details and recovery
requirements depend on the actual implementation.

## 3.11 Local Storage, Device Changes, and Data Loss

If the app stores notes locally on the device, the notes may be affected
by browser or application storage removal, a device reset, loss of the
device, browser-profile changes, or other storage-related problems.

Before clearing browser data, uninstalling or resetting an application,
switching devices, or performing maintenance that may remove local data:

1.  Review the current backup instructions.
2.  Create an appropriate backup if supported.
3.  Confirm that the backup file exists and is accessible.
4.  Follow the documented restoration procedure when moving to another
    device.
5.  Verify the restored notes before relying on the new setup.

Do not assume that notes automatically synchronize across devices unless
a supported synchronization feature is explicitly available and
configured.

## 3.12 Privacy and Responsible Use

Notes can contain personal, confidential, or commercially sensitive
information. Users are responsible for deciding what to store and for
protecting access to their devices and backup files.

Recommended practices:

-   Keep the device's operating-system lock enabled.
-   Avoid storing unnecessary identity documents or highly sensitive
    personal information in ordinary notes.
-   Do not use a note as a substitute for a secure password manager.
-   Avoid including another person's private information without a valid
    reason.
-   Review a note before exporting or sharing it.
-   Keep backup files away from public folders and shared devices.
-   Remove obsolete sensitive information when it is no longer needed,
    while considering that copies may remain in backups.

Local storage can give users direct control over their data, but it does
not eliminate all privacy or security risks.

## 3.13 Troubleshooting

### A Note Is Missing

Check whether you are using the correct device, browser, application
profile, category, or search term. If notes are stored locally, they may
not appear on another device automatically.

### A Note Appears Empty or Does Not Show the Latest Edit

Reopen the note and check whether the change was saved. Avoid repeatedly
editing the same note until you understand whether the latest content
was retained. If a backup exists, review the documented recovery
procedure before restoring it.

### A Note Is Masked and Its Contents Are Not Visible

Use the relevant reveal or unlock control shown by the app. If access
depends on an application setting or passcode, follow the app's recovery
instructions rather than assuming the contents can be recovered without
it.

### A Backup Does Not Contain the Expected Note

Confirm what the export includes and whether the backup was created
after the note was saved. Consult the backup and restoration
documentation for supported data coverage and limitations.

### Notes Are Unavailable After Clearing Storage or Changing Devices

Local data may have been removed or may remain on the original device.
Check for a valid backup and follow the documented restoration process.
Recovery is not guaranteed if no usable backup exists.

### A Search Does Not Find a Note

Try a shorter or more distinctive keyword, check the spelling, and
review any active category or filter. Search behavior depends on the
implementation of the installed version.

## 3.14 Limitations and Important Notes

-   The availability of note creation, editing, masking, tagging,
    searching, exporting, and backup depends on the app's implemented
    features and version.
-   A visual masking control should not be assumed to provide
    encryption.
-   Local storage is not a substitute for independent backups.
-   A note is not automatically a financial transaction, a notification,
    or a synchronized record.
-   Users should verify the behavior of the installed version before
    storing important information.
-   Do not use the vault as a replacement for professional legal,
    accounting, tax, or financial advice.
-   Do not assume that deleted notes can be recovered or that every note
    is included in every backup.

## 3.15 Important Disclaimer

The Personal Notes & Private Vault is intended for general note-taking
and organization. Users are responsible for protecting their devices,
choosing what information to store, and handling exported files
securely.

The presence of a private workspace or note-masking control does not
guarantee complete confidentiality, encryption, permanent storage, or
recovery after data loss. Before storing important or sensitive
information, review the application's actual privacy controls,
local-storage behavior, and backup limitations.

Feature names, screens, and behavior may change between versions. Always
follow the instructions shown in the installed version.

## 3.16 Summary

The **Personal Notes & Private Vault** provides a dedicated place for
general notes and planning information separate from routine financial
records. Clear titles, consistent organization, careful editing,
responsible handling of sensitive information, and regular backups can
make the workspace more useful.

Before storing important or confidential information, understand the
app's privacy controls, local-storage behavior, and backup limitations.
