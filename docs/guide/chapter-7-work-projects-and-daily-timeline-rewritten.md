# Chapter 7: Work Projects & Daily Timeline

## 7.1 Introduction

The **Work Projects & Daily Timeline** tools are intended to help you
organize professional activities, project progress, work hours, daily
tasks, and personal reflections in one place. Clear records can make it
easier to remember what was completed, identify unfinished work, review
time spent, and plan what to do next.

This chapter explains a practical way to use project records and daily
timeline entries, maintain consistent information, review older records,
and avoid common mistakes.

**Important:** These tools are for organization and record-keeping. They
do not automatically verify client agreements, prove attendance, or
replace an employer's official timesheet unless a specific documented
workflow in your installed version supports that purpose.

The exact fields, buttons, calculations, and report options may vary by
version. Follow the controls actually available in your installed
version of Daily Khata Pro.

## 7.2 Understanding Work Projects and Daily Timeline

This section covers two related kinds of records:

-   **Work-project records:** Information about a project, client,
    deliverable, task, progress, and time spent.
-   **Daily timeline records:** A dated record of important activities,
    routines, daily highlights, and personal reflections.

They can support different questions:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Record type                         Questions it may help you answer
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Work project                        What work is planned or underway?
                                      What has been completed? What
                                      remains pending?

  Work progress note                  What was done during a visit or
                                      work session? What is the next
                                      action?

  Work-time record                    How much time did I enter for a
                                      task, if time tracking is
                                      supported?

  Daily timeline                      What important activities happened
                                      on a particular day?

  Daily reflection                    What should I remember or
                                      prioritize tomorrow?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

These records can complement each other, but they are not necessarily
connected automatically. Check your installed version before assuming
that an entry in one area updates another feature.

## 7.3 Creating a Work Project Record

A project record can help keep related work information together. Where
the available fields support it, consider recording:

-   Project or task name.
-   Client, employer, or business name.
-   Work address or project location, when relevant.
-   Start date or planned deadline.
-   Task or deliverable description.
-   Work status.
-   Time spent or billable hours.
-   Project-related costs, if supported.
-   Progress notes, pending work, and the next action.

Use a descriptive project name that will still make sense weeks or
months later. For example, `Office lighting repair — Site A` is more
informative than `Work` or `Job 1`.

Do not assume every field listed here exists in every version. Use the
fields available in the application and keep important details in an
appropriate separate record if necessary.

## 7.4 Suggested Project Statuses

If your version provides a status field, use the available options
consistently. A simple status approach might be:

1.  **Planned:** The work has been identified but has not started.
2.  **In progress:** Work has started and is not yet complete.
3.  **Waiting:** Progress depends on a client, supplier, approval, or
    another task.
4.  **Completed:** The agreed task or deliverable has been completed.
5.  **Cancelled:** The task will not proceed.

These are suggested meanings, not a guarantee that the app contains
these exact status choices.

A status should describe the real situation. Do not mark work as
completed simply because a visit has ended if important agreed work
remains unfinished. If the app does not provide statuses, describe
progress in the available notes.

## 7.5 Recording Work Progress

After completing a meaningful task or reaching a milestone, update the
project record if the relevant controls are available.

A useful progress note answers three questions:

1.  **What was done?** Describe the work or deliverable.
2.  **What remains?** Record unfinished work, issues, or dependencies.
3.  **What happens next?** Write the next action, if known.

Keep notes factual and concise. Record what actually happened rather
than what was originally planned.

### Example progress note

-   **Project:** Shop electrical maintenance
-   **Work completed:** Checked the lighting circuit and replaced a
    faulty switch
-   **Time spent:** 60 minutes
-   **Status:** In progress, if further work remains
-   **Pending item:** Confirm the remaining items during the next visit
-   **Next action:** Arrange or complete the follow-up visit

This is an example of a clear record, not a required format. Enter
accurate details based on the work actually performed.

## 7.6 Recording Work Hours and Billable Time

If the application supports time or billable-hour tracking, record time
as accurately and consistently as possible.

Keep these concepts separate:

-   **Time worked:** Time spent doing the task.
-   **Billable time:** Time that may be chargeable under the relevant
    client agreement.
-   **Travel or waiting time:** Time spent travelling or waiting, where
    relevant and supported.
-   **Payment received:** Money actually received, if recorded in a
    financial section.
-   **Amount pending:** Money still due, if the application supports
    this information.

For example, you might spend 90 minutes at a site but have an agreement
that treats travel time separately. Record the relevant information
according to the available fields and your agreement. Do not assume the
app automatically determines what is billable.

Recording work hours does not necessarily create an invoice, collect
payment, or update income records. If you need to track both work
performed and money received, check whether separate entries are
required in the project and financial areas.

Before using time records for a client invoice, payroll, or formal
report, review them carefully and follow the applicable agreement or
workplace requirements.

## 7.7 Recording Project Locations and Client Details

If the app provides fields for a client, employer, or work location, use
only the information needed to identify the job.

For example:

-   `Client A — Office lighting repair`
-   `Site B — Maintenance visit`
-   `Project 104 — Installation follow-up`

A complete street address may be useful for some jobs, but it may not be
necessary for every record. Avoid storing sensitive information that is
not needed for your work.

Be careful when sharing screenshots, exports, or reports that contain
client names, addresses, phone numbers, or confidential project details.

## 7.8 Using the Daily Timeline

A daily timeline can help you keep a dated record of important
activities and routines.

Depending on the fields available, an entry may include:

-   Date and time.
-   Activity or event.
-   Work or personal category.
-   Short description.
-   Completion status, if supported.
-   Reflection or follow-up note.

Use the timeline for information you are likely to need later. You do
not need to record every minute of the day. A few clear and meaningful
entries are often easier to maintain and review than an unnecessarily
detailed log.

### Example daily timeline

  Time       Activity
  ---------- ------------------------------------------------
  10:00 AM   Opened the shop
  11:30 AM   Travelled to a work site
  12:00 PM   Completed a switch-installation check
  1:00 PM    Recorded remaining work and the next follow-up

Use your actual date and times. This example does not mean that the
application automatically detects activities, tracks your location, or
verifies timestamps.

## 7.9 Daily Routines and Reflections

If the timeline supports routine notes or personal reflections, you can
use it to record habits, planned work, learning activities, or an
end-of-day review.

Useful prompts include:

-   What was my main task today?
-   What did I complete?
-   What remains pending?
-   What should I prioritize tomorrow?
-   What went well?
-   What could I improve next time?

Keep reflections practical and respectful of your privacy. Store only
the personal information you need and are comfortable keeping on the
device.

A daily record does not have to be long to be useful. A short note such
as `Completed site inspection; prepare material list for follow-up` may
be easier to review than a long paragraph.

## 7.10 Mood and Energy Tracking

The documentation interface describes a mood and energy tracking module.
If this feature is available in your installed version, it may help you
record a simple personal observation about your day.

Use any labels or scales provided by the application consistently. For
example, if the feature offers suitable choices, you might note whether
your energy felt low, normal, or high.

Mood and energy entries are personal reflections, not medical
assessments or diagnoses. A short record does not prove why a particular
feeling occurred and should not be treated as a substitute for
professional support when needed.

If you add optional context, such as workload or sleep routine, keep it
factual. Avoid drawing strong conclusions from only a few entries.

## 7.11 Reviewing Work and Daily Records Together

The interface describes a combined report view for reviewing work
activity alongside other information. If your version supports it, you
may be able to review patterns such as:

-   Which projects remain unfinished.
-   Which tasks repeatedly require follow-up.
-   How entered work time is distributed across projects.
-   Whether planned daily tasks are being completed.
-   Which notes should be carried forward to the next day.

A relationship between two records does not necessarily mean one caused
the other. For example, a day with more work hours and lower energy does
not by itself establish why the energy level changed.

Use these records as a planning aid, not as an automatic performance
assessment or a definitive explanation of personal patterns.

## 7.12 A Practical Daily Workflow

A simple routine can help keep records current without creating too much
administrative work.

### Start of the day

-   Review active projects and pending tasks.
-   Identify the most important work to complete.
-   Check any deadlines or follow-ups that are relevant.

### During work

-   Update a project after a meaningful task or milestone.
-   Record time when the feature is available and it is useful.
-   Add a brief note about unfinished work or dependencies.
-   Keep the record focused on the actual work performed.

### End of the day

-   Add a short daily timeline entry or reflection.
-   Review tasks that remain pending.
-   Identify the next action for unfinished projects.
-   Correct obvious errors while details are still fresh.
-   Follow the application's backup guidance for important records.

This is a suggested workflow, not a requirement. Choose a level of
detail you can maintain consistently.

## 7.13 Editing Incorrect Entries

If an entry contains a mistake and editing is available:

1.  Open the relevant project or timeline record.
2.  Locate the incorrect entry.
3.  Review the date, time, description, and other relevant fields.
4.  Make only the necessary correction.
5.  Save the change.
6.  Confirm that the updated record is displayed correctly.

If deletion is the only available option, consider whether you need to
preserve the original information before removing it. Avoid changing
historical records to misrepresent work performed or create an
inaccurate account of events.

The exact editing and deletion controls depend on the installed version.
Use only the actions actually available.

## 7.14 Search, Organization, and Naming

If search or filters are available, use consistent terms for projects,
clients, and locations.

Helpful practices include:

-   Use descriptive project names.
-   Choose one consistent name for each client or site.
-   Include dates where they help distinguish recurring work.
-   Keep notes focused on the task.
-   Avoid unnecessary duplicate entries.
-   Review older active projects periodically.
-   Mark completed work when the relevant feature supports it.

If the app does not provide a particular filter, status, or search
option, do not assume it exists. Use the available controls and naming
conventions.

## 7.15 Privacy and Data Protection

Work records can contain client names, addresses, schedules, and
business details. Daily reflections may contain personal information as
well.

To reduce unnecessary exposure:

-   Store only information you need.
-   Avoid entering passwords, payment-card details, or other highly
    sensitive information in general notes.
-   Protect access to your device and application.
-   Be careful when sharing screenshots, exports, or reports.
-   Review exported files before sending them to another person.
-   Maintain backups using the application's supported backup process.

If records are stored locally, they may be affected by device loss,
storage problems, or removal of application data. Local storage should
not be treated as an independent backup. Check the app's documented
backup and restore options for your version.

## 7.16 Common Problems and Troubleshooting

### A project or timeline entry is missing

Check whether the entry was saved, whether the correct section or date
is open, and whether a search term or filter is hiding it.

### The recorded time is incorrect

Open the entry and review the date, start/end times, or duration fields
that are actually available. Correct the record if editing is supported.

### A project appears unfinished

Review the status and outstanding-task notes. Mark work completed only
when the agreed work is actually complete.

### A report does not show expected information

Confirm that the relevant entries exist and that the report includes the
correct date range or filters, if those options are provided.

### Work hours do not match a payment received

Work performed and money received are different records. Review the
relevant time entry, agreement, invoice, and payment record. Do not
assume that the app automatically links these items unless that feature
is documented.

### Data is missing after changing devices or clearing storage

Check whether a supported backup exists and follow the application's
backup-and-restore instructions. Do not assume locally stored records
automatically transfer to another device.

## 7.17 Good Record-Keeping Practices

For reliable and useful project and timeline records:

-   Enter information as soon as reasonably practical.
-   Use consistent project names and categories.
-   Keep progress notes specific and factual.
-   Record what was actually completed.
-   Separate time worked from payment received.
-   Review pending tasks regularly.
-   Correct mistakes transparently.
-   Avoid collecting unnecessary sensitive information.
-   Back up important records using supported methods.
-   Verify any report before using it for billing, employment, or formal
    business purposes.

## 7.18 Important Limitations

Work Projects & Daily Timeline is intended to help organize information.
Unless explicitly supported by the installed application, do not assume
it:

-   Automatically tracks every activity or verifies attendance.
-   Independently validates hours worked.
-   Sends invoices or collects payments.
-   Replaces an employer's official timesheet.
-   Provides professional health or productivity assessments.
-   Synchronizes records across devices automatically.
-   Guarantees the accuracy of user-entered information.

Check the features available in your current version before relying on a
particular workflow.

## 7.20 Chapter Summary

The **Work Projects & Daily Timeline** tools can help you organize
project details, work progress, time records, daily activities, and
personal reflections in one place. Their usefulness depends on clear,

Use the tools available in your installed version, review records

maintain independent backups of important information.
