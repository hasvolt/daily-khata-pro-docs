# Chapter 8: Financial Goals & Milestones

## 8.1 Overview

The **Financial Goals & Milestones** section helps you define a financial target, organize the purpose behind it, and review recorded progress over time.

A financial goal can represent an emergency reserve, a planned purchase, education or training costs, travel, equipment, home improvements, a business expense, or another personal objective. Instead of keeping every plan in memory, you can use a clearly named goal to maintain a record of what you are working toward.

This feature is a **planning and record-keeping tool**. It does not guarantee that a goal will be achieved, move money between accounts automatically, or confirm how much money is actually held by a bank or another financial institution.

Depending on the version of Daily Khata Pro installed, the available goal controls may include a target amount, contributions or deposits, a related fund category, a target date, progress information, milestones, notes, or a completion status. Some options described in this chapter may not appear in every version. Use only the controls that are actually available in your app.

### What this chapter covers

- Understanding financial goals and milestones.
- Choosing a clear goal name and target amount.
- Linking a goal to a fund category, if supported.
- Recording contributions or deposits, if supported.
- Understanding progress amounts and percentages.
- Reviewing milestones and target dates.
- Updating, completing, archiving, or deleting goals where those options exist.
- Troubleshooting common record-keeping issues.
- Understanding the feature's limitations and privacy considerations.

## 8.2 What Is a Financial Goal?

A **financial goal** is a target amount you want to set aside for a particular purpose. A goal gives a financial plan a clear name and a figure that you can review later.

Examples include:

- **Emergency Reserve:** money planned for unexpected needs.
- **Equipment Purchase:** a laptop, phone, electrical tool, or other work equipment.
- **Education or Training:** course fees, books, certification, or training expenses.
- **Travel or Family Event:** a planned trip or event-related cost.
- **Home Maintenance:** repairs, improvements, or replacement equipment.
- **Business Expense:** a planned purchase or operating requirement.
- **Long-Term Savings Reserve:** a broader savings objective.

### Choose a specific name

Use a title that explains the goal's purpose. For example, **Emergency Reserve** is more informative than simply **Savings** when the money is intended for emergencies.

If you maintain several goals, descriptive names make them easier to distinguish. Avoid creating multiple goals with almost identical names unless they represent genuinely different purposes.

### Keep separate purposes separate

Where practical, create separate goals for major objectives. For example, a planned equipment purchase and an emergency reserve have different purposes. Keeping them separate can make it easier to review each plan independently.

The app's goal records do not replace your own judgment about which financial needs should take priority.

## 8.3 Define a Goal Target

The **target amount** is the total amount you intend to set aside for the goal. Enter it carefully and check the displayed currency and number format before saving.

When creating or editing a goal, consider the following details where the installed version supports them:

1. **Goal name:** A short, recognizable title.
2. **Target amount:** The total amount you intend to work toward.
3. **Target date:** A realistic date, if this field is available.
4. **Related fund or category:** A category that matches the goal's purpose, if available.
5. **Notes:** Additional context about the purpose or how you estimated the target, if supported.

### Before saving the target

- Check that the amount has the correct number of digits.
- Check the decimal point, if you use one.
- Confirm the displayed currency.
- Make sure the target represents the full intended amount rather than only the next contribution.
- Review the goal name to ensure it is easy to recognize later.

**Example:** If you intend to set aside ₹20,000 for a work laptop, the target amount would be ₹20,000. It would not be the amount you plan to contribute this week.

Not every version of Daily Khata Pro will display all the fields listed above. Follow the actual controls available in your installed version.

## 8.4 Link a Goal to a Fund Category

If the app provides a fund-category selection, you may associate a goal with a category that matches its purpose.

Examples:

- An emergency reserve may be associated with an emergency fund category.
- A planned computer purchase may be associated with an equipment or personal fund category.
- A longer-term objective may be associated with a savings-planning category.

### Why a category can help

A related category can help you organize a goal alongside other financial records. It may make the purpose of the goal easier to understand when reviewing your records.

### Important distinction: category link versus actual money movement

Linking a goal to a category does **not necessarily**:

- Transfer money between bank accounts.
- Create a bank deposit.
- Open a separate savings account.
- Move money from one fund to another.
- Guarantee that the target amount is available.

A category or goal link may be an organizational relationship only. Check the app's actual behavior before assuming that linking a category represents a real-world financial transaction.

## 8.5 Record Deposits and Contributions

If the goal includes a **Deposit**, **Contribution**, or similar option, use it to record an amount intended to count toward the goal.

A careful workflow is:

1. Open the relevant goal.
2. Review the goal name and target amount.
3. Check the currently recorded progress.
4. Select the available deposit or contribution control.
5. Enter the amount accurately.
6. Review the amount before confirming or saving.
7. Reopen or review the goal to check the updated progress.

Use this workflow only if the relevant controls exist in your version of the app.

### Record the correct amount

Record only the amount you intend the app to count toward that goal. If you are entering a contribution of ₹2,000 toward a ₹20,000 goal, do not enter the full target amount again unless the app specifically asks for the total amount saved to date.

### Avoid double-counting

The same money may be represented in more than one part of a personal finance system. For example, you might record an income transaction and also record a contribution toward a goal.

Before entering the same amount in multiple places, understand what each record represents. A goal contribution may be a separate tracking entry; it may not automatically create or link to an income, expense, bank transaction, or fund transfer. Do not assume that the app links these records unless it explicitly indicates that it does.

### Correct an incorrect contribution

If you enter an incorrect amount, use the available edit or correction control, if provided. Then review the goal's progress again. If the app does not provide a correction option, consult its current instructions before making additional entries that could create further confusion.

## 8.6 Understand Goal Progress

The app may show progress using a recorded amount, a progress bar, a percentage, or estimated completion information. The exact display depends on the features and calculation rules in the installed version.

For a simple target, the progress percentage can be understood using this formula:

**Progress percentage = (Recorded amount toward the goal ÷ Target amount) × 100**

### Example: ₹20,000 goal

Suppose the target amount is ₹20,000 and the amount recorded toward the goal is ₹5,000.

- Target amount: ₹20,000
- Recorded amount: ₹5,000
- Calculation: ₹5,000 ÷ ₹20,000 × 100
- Progress: **25%**
- Remaining amount, using these figures: **₹15,000**

This example explains the arithmetic. It does not guarantee that the app uses precisely the same display or calculation rules in every version.

### How to interpret progress

Keep the following points in mind:

- Progress is only as accurate as the entries used to calculate it.
- A displayed amount records what the application knows; it is not independent verification of money held in a bank or elsewhere.
- If you change the target amount, the displayed progress percentage may change even if the recorded contribution stays the same.
- If the recorded amount reaches the target, review the figures before marking the goal complete, if a completion option exists.
- If the recorded amount exceeds the target, check how the app represents over-target progress.
- If a contribution is missing or duplicated, the displayed progress may not reflect your intended plan.

### Check the remaining amount

A simple way to review a goal is:

**Remaining amount = Target amount − Recorded amount toward the goal**

For example, if the target is ₹24,000 and the recorded amount is ₹9,000, the remaining amount is ₹15,000. This is a basic calculation example; use the app's displayed values and your own records to confirm your actual position.

## 8.7 Set Milestones and Review Dates

A large goal may be easier to review when you divide it into smaller checkpoints. If the application supports milestones or target dates, use those options to organize your plan.

### Example: ₹24,000 planned purchase

You could use the following illustrative checkpoints:

| Checkpoint | Recorded amount |
|---|---:|
| Milestone 1 | ₹6,000 |
| Milestone 2 | ₹12,000 |
| Milestone 3 | ₹18,000 |
| Final target | ₹24,000 |

These are examples, not automatic recommendations. Choose checkpoints that suit your own circumstances.

### What to review at a milestone

When reviewing a goal, consider:

- The original target amount.
- The amount recorded so far.
- The amount remaining.
- The target date, if configured.
- Any milestone information available in the app.
- Whether the purpose or priority of the goal has changed.
- Whether the plan remains realistic for your current circumstances.

### Treat estimated dates as planning aids

An estimated completion date is not a promise. Contributions may vary, and the app may not account for every change in income, expenses, or other real-world circumstances. Do not rely on an estimated date as a guarantee that funds will be available by that time.

If your plans change, update the goal only through controls supported by the installed version and review the resulting progress display.

## 8.8 Review and Update Existing Goals

Review goals periodically so that the information remains useful and understandable.

When opening an existing goal, check the following details where available:

- Goal name and purpose.
- Target amount.
- Recorded contributions or deposits.
- Current progress amount or percentage.
- Linked category or fund.
- Target date or milestone details.
- Notes and status.

### When the target changes

If the estimated cost of a purchase changes, you may need to update the target. Use the available edit control, if supported. After making a change, check the displayed progress again because a different target can change the percentage.

For example, if a target changes from ₹20,000 to ₹25,000 while the recorded contribution remains ₹5,000, the contribution represents a different percentage of the new target.

### When the goal is completed

If the app provides a completion or status option, review the recorded amount before using it. Keep the record for reference if the app supports that workflow and you want to retain the history.

Reaching a target in the app does not itself confirm that a purchase was made or that a separate account contains the money.

### When the goal is no longer needed

If the goal is no longer relevant, check whether the app offers an archive, completion, or delete option. Consider whether you may need the record later before deleting it.

Recovery depends on the app's actual deletion, trash, backup, and restore features. Do not assume that a deleted goal can always be recovered.

## 8.9 Practical Goal-Planning Guidelines

The following practices can help keep goal records clear:

1. **Use a separate goal for each major purpose.** This can make progress easier to review.
2. **Choose descriptive names.** Make it clear what the goal is for.
3. **Set targets based on your own needs.** The app does not determine what amount is suitable for you.
4. **Review progress periodically.** Update the record when your plans change.
5. **Keep emergency needs distinct from optional purchases where practical.** This is an organizational suggestion, not a required rule.
6. **Avoid counting the same money twice.** Understand whether each entry represents a transaction, a contribution, or a planning record.
7. **Check amounts after editing.** A target change can affect the displayed percentage.
8. **Maintain backups of important application data.** Follow the backup instructions for your installed version.
9. **Verify important figures against your own records.** This is especially important before making a significant financial decision.
10. **Do not treat a progress bar as proof of available funds.** Confirm actual balances using your own financial records.

These are general organization practices, not individualized financial advice.

## 8.10 Common Questions and Troubleshooting

### The goal progress looks incorrect. What should I check?

Review the target amount and all recorded contributions. Check for:

- An incorrect target amount.
- A contribution entered with the wrong number of digits.
- Duplicate entries.
- A missing contribution.
- A recent change to the target.
- A difference between a goal contribution and a separate financial transaction.

If the result still appears incorrect, consult the current app instructions or report the issue with the steps needed to reproduce it.

### Does creating a goal move money into a bank account?

Not necessarily. A goal-tracking feature may only record a target and progress inside the application. Do not treat it as a bank transfer or actual deposit unless the app explicitly provides and confirms that functionality.

### Does linking a fund category guarantee that the goal is funded?

No. A category or goal link may be an organizational feature only. Verify actual balances through the relevant financial institution or independent financial records.

### Can I change a goal target later?

Use the goal's available edit controls, if supported. After changing the target, review the displayed progress and remaining amount.

### What if I reach the target?

Check the recorded amount and use a completion or status option if one is available. You may retain the record for reference if the application supports that workflow.

### What if the recorded amount is higher than the target?

Review the target and contribution entries. If the entries are correct, check how the installed version represents progress beyond the target. Do not assume that an over-target display means money has been transferred or verified.

### What if I delete a goal by mistake?

Recovery depends on the application's available deletion, trash, backup, and restore features. Do not assume a deleted goal can always be recovered. Review the relevant recovery instructions before changing important records.

### Why does progress change after I edit the target?

The percentage depends on both the recorded amount and the target amount. If the target changes while the recorded amount stays the same, the progress percentage may change.

### Why does the goal amount not match my bank balance?

A goal record may represent a plan or a manually entered contribution rather than a live bank balance. Compare the goal with your actual financial records and check whether the app supports any explicit account integration in your installed version.

## 8.11 Data Accuracy and Privacy

Financial goals can reveal personal information about planned purchases, savings, future expenses, or business needs. Protect access to your device and application, and avoid sharing screenshots or exported records that reveal information you want to keep private.

Remember:

- Goal records are only as reliable as the information entered.
- A displayed progress figure is not independent verification of funds.
- A goal record should not be assumed to be encrypted or protected in a particular way unless the app's current documentation confirms it.
- Maintain independent backups according to the application's backup instructions.
- Before relying on a figure for a major decision, verify it against your own financial records.
- If you share a device, consider whether other people can access the application or its stored data.

## 8.12 Important Notice

Daily Khata Pro provides tools for organizing financial information. It does not guarantee savings, investment returns, or completion of a financial goal. Any target, progress display, milestone, or estimated date should be treated as a planning aid rather than a prediction.

Users are responsible for checking their entries and deciding how to manage their money. For decisions involving taxes, investments, borrowing, or other complex financial matters, consult an appropriately qualified professional where needed.
