# Chapter 17: Multi-Purpose Financial Planning Calculators

## 1. Purpose of This Chapter

Daily Khata Pro includes multiple calculator modes for everyday arithmetic, financial planning, tax estimates, pricing calculations, and money management. Each calculator is designed for a different type of task, so choosing the correct mode and entering the correct values are important.

This chapter explains the calculator modes, the information they may require, how to review the results, and what to keep in mind before using a calculation in a financial record or decision.

> **Important:** Results depend on the values entered, the assumptions used, and the calculator’s implementation. These tools are intended for general calculations and planning. They do not replace official lender schedules, current exchange-rate quotes, professional tax guidance, or personalized financial advice.

## 2. Overview of Calculator Modes

To open the calculator area, select **Calculator** from the bottom navigation bar, then choose the mode that matches your task.

The calculator menu includes:

1. **Standard Calc** — everyday arithmetic and general calculations.
2. **Currency / Forex** — currency conversion using a rate available or entered in the calculator.
3. **Loan EMI** — an estimate of periodic loan repayments.
4. **SIP & Wealth** — an estimate of how recurring contributions or a lump sum may grow under an assumed return.
5. **Smart Fund Split** — divides an amount among configured funds or categories using allocation percentages.
6. **GST Tax** — GST-related arithmetic where supported.
7. **Discount & Margin** — pricing, discount, markup, and margin calculations where supported.
8. **Goal & Inflation** — estimates a future goal cost using an assumed inflation rate.

The exact fields, labels, available options, and displayed results can vary by calculator and application version. If a field described here is not present in your version, use the controls actually provided on the screen.

## 3. Standard Calc

### What it does

Use **Standard Calc** for everyday arithmetic and general calculations, such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, and other operations supported by the calculator.

It can be useful for checking a bill total, adding several expenses, estimating a work charge, or verifying a number before entering it into another part of the app.

### How to use it

1. Open **Calculator**.
2. Select **Standard Calc**.
3. Enter the numbers you want to calculate.
4. Select the required mathematical operation or operations.
5. Review the expression and result.
6. Check the result before copying it into a financial record, invoice, or another calculation.

### Good practices

- Check the order of operations when a calculation contains several operations.
- Confirm decimal places and units before using the result elsewhere.
- For money calculations, check whether rounding is appropriate for your purpose.
- Re-enter the calculation if you are unsure which values or operations were used.
- A calculator result is only as accurate as the values entered.

### Example

If a job includes three charges of ₹500, ₹750, and ₹250, the total is:

`₹500 + ₹750 + ₹250 = ₹1,500`

This is a simple arithmetic example. If the charges involve tax, discounts, or other adjustments, calculate and review those separately or use the relevant calculator mode.

## 4. Currency / Forex

### What it does

Use **Currency / Forex** for currency conversion or exchange-rate calculations when a relevant exchange rate is available or entered in the calculator.

### Typical workflow

1. Select **Currency / Forex**.
2. Choose or enter the source and target currencies, as supported by the screen.
3. Enter the amount to convert.
4. Check the exchange rate being used.
5. Review the converted amount and currency labels.
6. If the amount is for a real payment or exchange, verify the rate and fees with the provider before relying on the result.

### Understanding the calculation

A simplified conversion is:

`Converted Amount = Original Amount × Exchange Rate`

For example, if a hypothetical rate is 1 unit of Currency A = 80 units of Currency B, then 10 units of Currency A would convert to 800 units of Currency B before any fees or provider adjustments.

This example illustrates the arithmetic only. It is not a current exchange-rate quote.

### Important notes

- Exchange rates change over time.
- If the app uses a manually entered rate, confirm that it is current for your purpose.
- Banks, card providers, payment services, and exchange providers may use different rates.
- Fees, spreads, taxes, and rounding may affect the amount actually received or paid.
- Do not assume that a calculated conversion is a guaranteed amount you will receive.
- Selecting a currency does not necessarily mean the app fetches a live rate or performs an automatic market conversion.

For an actual transfer, purchase, or business invoice, confirm the applicable rate, fee, and settlement amount with the provider.

## 5. Loan EMI

### What it does

Use **Loan EMI** to estimate periodic loan repayments based on the loan amount, interest rate, and repayment period.

EMI commonly means **Equated Monthly Instalment**. A typical EMI includes a principal component and an interest component. The proportions generally change over the repayment period for a standard amortizing loan.

### Typical inputs

- Principal or loan amount
- Annual interest rate
- Loan tenure

Additional fields or displayed results may vary by calculator version.

### How to use it

1. Select **Loan EMI**.
2. Enter the loan amount.
3. Enter the interest rate in the format requested by the calculator.
4. Enter the repayment tenure using the units requested by the screen.
5. Review the estimated instalment.
6. If shown, review total interest and total repayment.
7. Change one input at a time to compare scenarios.
8. Compare the estimate with the lender’s official repayment schedule before making a commitment.

### Standard EMI formula

For a standard fixed-rate loan with monthly instalments:

`EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n − 1)`

Where:

- `P` = principal amount
- `r` = monthly interest rate expressed as a decimal
- `n` = total number of monthly instalments

For example, an annual interest rate of 12% corresponds to a nominal monthly rate of 1% when divided by 12, expressed as `0.01` in the formula. This explains the formula’s rate format; it does not calculate a specific loan offer.

### How to compare scenarios

You can compare different loan amounts, interest rates, or tenures to understand how the estimated EMI changes. Keep the other inputs unchanged when you want to understand the effect of one variable.

A longer tenure may reduce the periodic instalment but can increase the total interest paid under many standard loan structures. Review both the instalment and the total repayment figure if the calculator provides them.

### Important limitations

Actual lender calculations may include processing fees, insurance, variable interest rates, special repayment conditions, rounding, or other terms not represented by a simple estimate. The lender’s official offer and repayment schedule should be treated as the authoritative source for that loan.

## 6. SIP & Wealth

### What it does

Use **SIP & Wealth** to estimate how recurring contributions or a lump-sum amount might grow over time under an assumed rate of return, where those options are provided.

SIP commonly refers to a **Systematic Investment Plan**, in which contributions are made at regular intervals. The calculator’s available options determine whether it models recurring contributions, a lump sum, or both.

### Typical inputs

- Recurring contribution or investment amount
- Initial lump sum, if supported
- Investment duration
- Assumed annual return

The exact fields and calculation assumptions depend on the version.

### How to use it

1. Select **SIP & Wealth**.
2. Enter the recurring contribution or lump-sum amount requested by the screen.
3. Enter the investment duration.
4. Enter the assumed annual return, if requested.
5. Review the projected value.
6. If displayed, compare the invested amount with the estimated growth.
7. Adjust the inputs to compare different scenarios.
8. Treat the result as a mathematical projection rather than a promise.

### Understanding a projection

A projection illustrates what could happen if the chosen assumptions held for the full period. It does not predict actual market performance.

For example, a projection based on a steady assumed annual return may look different from real investment performance, which can vary from year to year and may include losses. The result can also depend on contribution timing, fees, taxes, and the method used to compound returns.

### Important notes

- Projections are estimates, not promises of future performance.
- Actual investment returns can be lower or higher than the assumed rate and may be negative.
- Fees, taxes, contribution timing, withdrawals, and market movements can affect actual outcomes.
- An assumed return should not be interpreted as a guaranteed or expected return for a particular investment.
- Do not use a single projection as the only basis for an investment decision.

For personal investment decisions, consider your goals, time horizon, ability to handle losses, and relevant professional guidance.

## 7. Smart Fund Split

### What it does

Use **Smart Fund Split** to calculate how an income amount or another total amount can be divided among configured funds or categories according to the allocation percentages set in the app.

It can help with planning how to distribute money among categories such as savings, household expenses, business costs, or other funds you have configured.

### How to use it

1. Confirm the fund names and allocation percentages in the relevant settings.
2. Open **Smart Fund Split**.
3. Enter the total income or amount you want to allocate.
4. Review the calculated amount for each fund.
5. Check whether the percentages total **100%** if you intend to distribute the entire amount.
6. Review any remaining or unallocated amount.
7. Confirm the figures before recording or applying the split.

### Basic formula

`Allocated Amount = Total Amount × Allocation Percentage ÷ 100`

For example, if you allocate 20% of ₹10,000 to a particular fund:

`₹10,000 × 20 ÷ 100 = ₹2,000`

This is an example of the calculation, not a recommendation about how your money should be allocated.

### Understanding allocation percentages

- If the percentages total 100%, the configured shares are intended to distribute the full amount.
- If the percentages total less than 100%, part of the amount may remain unallocated.
- If the percentages total more than 100%, the calculated shares may exceed the original amount.
- The app may handle incomplete or excessive allocations according to its design, so check the displayed result rather than assuming a particular behaviour.
- Small rounding differences can occur when percentages produce fractional currency amounts.

### Good practices

Review your allocation settings whenever your budgeting priorities change. Remember that a calculated allocation is a plan or split; it does not necessarily move money between bank accounts or prove that a real transfer occurred.

## 8. GST Tax

### What it does

Use **GST Tax** for GST-related arithmetic, such as calculating a tax amount or working backward from a tax-inclusive amount, where those calculation options are available.

### How to use it

1. Select **GST Tax**.
2. Enter the taxable amount or total amount as requested.
3. Select or enter the applicable GST rate.
4. Check whether the calculation uses an amount before tax or an amount that already includes tax.
5. Review the calculated tax and total.
6. Verify the figures before using them in an invoice, quote, or business record.

### Tax-exclusive and tax-inclusive amounts

A tax-exclusive amount is the amount before tax is added. A tax-inclusive amount already contains the tax.

These are different calculation situations. Before interpreting the result, confirm which type of amount the calculator expects. Entering a tax-inclusive amount into a field intended for a pre-tax amount can produce an incorrect result.

### Important notes

- The correct GST treatment depends on the transaction, product or service, place of supply, registration status, and applicable rules.
- A calculator can perform arithmetic but cannot independently confirm that a tax rate or tax treatment is legally correct.
- Verify tax details against current official guidance or with a qualified tax professional when needed.
- Do not assume that the calculator automatically determines whether a transaction is taxable or which rate applies.
- A correct arithmetic result does not by itself establish that an invoice meets all applicable requirements.

## 9. Discount & Margin

### What it does

Use **Discount & Margin** for common pricing calculations, such as estimating a discounted price or reviewing the relationship between cost, selling price, and margin, where supported by the calculator.

This can be useful when preparing a quote, reviewing product pricing, or estimating a selling price. Confirm the meaning of each input field because similar terms can refer to different bases.

### How to use it

1. Select **Discount & Margin**.
2. Enter the price, cost, discount, or margin values requested by the screen.
3. Check whether a percentage is applied to the original price, cost, or selling price.
4. Review the resulting price or percentage.
5. Verify the final figures before using them in a quote, invoice, or product listing.
6. Consider whether other costs need to be included in your pricing analysis.

### Discount example

If an item’s original price is ₹1,000 and a discount of 10% is applied to that original price:

`Discount = ₹1,000 × 10 ÷ 100 = ₹100`

`Price After Discount = ₹1,000 − ₹100 = ₹900`

This example assumes the discount is calculated from the original price and excludes tax or other charges.

### Markup versus margin

**Markup** and **margin** are different:

- **Markup** is generally calculated as profit divided by cost.
- **Margin** is generally calculated as profit divided by selling price.

For example, if an item costs ₹80 and sells for ₹100, the profit before other costs is ₹20.

- Markup = ₹20 ÷ ₹80 = 25%
- Margin = ₹20 ÷ ₹100 = 20%

Because the base amount differs, the percentages are not interchangeable.

### Good practices

- Confirm whether the calculator expects a percentage or a decimal.
- Check which amount the percentage uses as its base.
- Consider taxes, delivery charges, payment fees, platform fees, labour, and other relevant costs when evaluating a real selling price.
- Do not assume that a displayed margin is net profit unless all relevant costs are included in the calculation.

## 10. Goal & Inflation

### What it does

Use **Goal & Inflation** to estimate how a target amount may change over time as prices rise. This can help with planning for future expenses such as education, equipment, home improvements, or another financial goal.

### Typical inputs

- Current goal amount
- Number of years until the goal
- Assumed annual inflation rate

Additional fields may be available depending on the calculator.

### How to use it

1. Select **Goal & Inflation**.
2. Enter the current estimated cost of the goal.
3. Enter the number of years until the goal.
4. Enter the assumed annual inflation rate.
5. Review the estimated future cost.
6. Adjust the timeline or inflation assumption to compare scenarios.
7. Use the result as a planning estimate rather than a guaranteed future price.

### Simplified formula

`Future Cost = Current Cost × (1 + Inflation Rate)^Years`

The inflation rate in this formula is expressed as a decimal. For example, 4% is written as `0.04`.

A goal costing ₹1,00,000 today would cost approximately ₹1,48,000 after 10 years at an assumed annual inflation rate of 4%. This is an illustration of the formula, not a forecast of actual future prices.

### Important notes

- Actual inflation varies across products, services, locations, and time periods.
- A general inflation rate may not match the cost changes for a specific goal.
- The estimate does not automatically tell you how much to save each month unless the calculator explicitly provides that calculation.
- A future-cost estimate is not a guarantee of what the goal will cost.

## 11. General Calculator Workflow

For any calculator mode, use a careful and repeatable process:

1. Open **Calculator** from the bottom navigation bar.
2. Choose the mode that matches your task.
3. Read the labels and instructions shown on the screen.
4. Enter the values carefully and confirm units.
5. Check the rate, percentage, currency, or time period being used.
6. Review the result and any assumptions.
7. For important calculations, verify the inputs and result independently.
8. Only then copy or use the result in a record, quote, invoice, or planning exercise.

Do not assume that a result has been saved to your financial records simply because it appears on the calculator screen. Use the relevant record or save function if you need to retain the information and that function is available.

## 12. Comparing Scenarios Responsibly

Many calculator modes are useful for comparing “what if” situations. To make a comparison meaningful, change one input at a time while keeping the other inputs the same.

Examples include:

- Comparing EMI estimates for different loan tenures.
- Comparing investment projections under different assumed return rates.
- Comparing a goal’s estimated future cost under different inflation assumptions.
- Comparing different discount or selling-price scenarios.
- Reviewing how a fund allocation changes when a percentage is updated.

Write down the assumptions behind each scenario. A result without its inputs can be difficult to interpret later.

For high-impact financial decisions, use conservative assumptions where appropriate and verify important figures with the relevant provider or professional.

## 13. Common Issues & Troubleshooting

### The result looks unexpectedly high or low

Check the amount, units, time period, percentage format, and whether the calculation uses a before-tax or after-tax amount. Review each input rather than changing several values at once.

### The result does not match another app or provider

Different tools may use different rounding rules, exchange rates, assumptions, or calculation methods. Compare the inputs and formula before deciding why the results differ. For a real loan, payment, tax matter, or currency exchange, the relevant provider’s confirmed terms may differ from a general estimate.

### The currency conversion seems wrong

Check the source currency, target currency, amount, and exchange rate. Confirm whether fees or provider-specific rates are included. If the rate is manually entered, verify that it is appropriate for the date and transaction.

### The EMI estimate differs from a lender’s figure

Review the principal, interest rate, tenure, rate format, and repayment frequency. The lender may include fees, insurance, rate changes, or other terms not represented in a simple EMI calculation. Use the lender’s official repayment schedule for the actual loan.

### An investment projection appears guaranteed

A projection depends on assumptions and is not a promise of future returns. Review the assumed rate, duration, contribution amount, and other inputs. Actual investment outcomes may be higher or lower and can include losses.

### The GST amount appears correct, but the invoice treatment is uncertain

The calculator checks arithmetic; it does not independently determine the legal tax treatment of a transaction. Verify the applicable rules and required invoice details separately.

### The discount or margin result seems inconsistent

Check whether the percentage is based on cost, original price, or selling price. Confirm which costs are included and whether the displayed value represents markup, margin, or discount.

### The future goal amount seems unrealistic

Review the current goal amount, number of years, and assumed inflation rate. Actual costs may rise faster or more slowly than the selected assumption, especially for specific goods or services.

## 15. Privacy and Recordkeeping

When using calculators, avoid entering unnecessary personal or confidential information. If you are testing an unfamiliar version, use sample values instead of real financial details.

If you need to keep a result for future reference, record the relevant inputs, assumptions, date, and result in an appropriate place. A displayed result should not be assumed to become a permanent financial record unless you use a feature that explicitly saves it.

When sharing a calculation with another person, include enough context to explain the result but avoid exposing account numbers, private client details, or other sensitive information.
