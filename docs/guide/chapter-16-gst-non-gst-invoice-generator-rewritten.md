# Chapter 16: GST & Non-GST Invoice Generator Tools

## 1. Purpose of This Chapter

Daily Khata Pro includes an invoice generator designed to help freelancers, service providers, small businesses, and independent professionals prepare structured business invoices.

The tool brings sender details, client information, invoice metadata, itemized billing, discounts, configurable tax fields, payment details, notes, and printable invoice layouts into one workflow. It can help users organize billing information and calculate the amount payable.

This chapter explains the available fields, how to prepare an invoice, how to review totals, and what to check before sharing an invoice with a client.

> **Important:** The invoice generator assists with invoice preparation and calculations. It does not by itself guarantee that an invoice complies with every applicable tax, accounting, or legal requirement. Verify the current rules that apply to your business before issuing an invoice.

## 2. Overview of the Invoice Generator

The Invoice Generator provides a structured interface for preparing invoices, calculating payable amounts, and maintaining business billing records.

Depending on the available interface and version, users can customize invoice details, choose supported currencies and templates, preview an invoice, save their work, and prepare a printable or PDF copy.

An invoice commonly records:

- Who is issuing the invoice
- Who is being billed
- What product, service, or work was supplied
- Quantity, unit, rate, discounts, and applicable tax
- The amount due
- Invoice date, due date, and payment terms
- How the client can make payment
- Additional notes and business terms

Before entering information, collect the relevant business and client details. This helps reduce mistakes and makes the final document easier to review.

## 3. Business Information (Sender)

The sender section identifies the business or individual issuing the invoice.

### Available fields

- Business Name / Store Name
- Tax ID / VAT / GSTIN (optional)
- Street Address, Area, and Landmark
- City
- State / Province / Region
- Postal / ZIP / Pincode
- Phone Number
- Email Address
- Business Logo

The interface also provides options to use previously saved business details, enter new details, and save business information for future invoices.

### How to complete this section

1. Enter the name that should appear on the invoice.
2. Add the business address and contact information relevant to the invoice.
3. Enter a tax registration number only when applicable and ensure it is accurate.
4. Add the business logo if you use one and the interface supports it.
5. Review the details before saving or printing.

### Practical guidance

Use consistent business details across invoices so clients can identify the sender. If you operate as an individual rather than a registered business, use the correct name and registration details for your situation. Do not enter a tax registration number that does not belong to you.

Because saved sender details may be reused, review them when your address, phone number, email, or registration information changes. Confirm the fields on each invoice rather than assuming saved information is always current.

## 4. Client Information (Bill To)

The client section identifies the person or organization responsible for the invoice.

### Available fields

- Client / Person Name
- Company Name (optional)
- Billing Address
- Tax ID / VAT / GSTIN (optional)
- Phone Number
- Email Address
- Different shipping address, where required

### How to complete this section

1. Enter the client’s name or the appropriate company name.
2. Add the billing address requested or required for the transaction.
3. Include a client tax ID only when relevant and supplied or confirmed by the client.
4. Add contact details when they are needed for business communication.
5. If goods are shipped to another address, use the separate shipping address option when applicable.

### Billing address versus shipping address

The billing address identifies the address associated with billing. A shipping address identifies where goods should be delivered. They may be the same, but they are not necessarily interchangeable.

For a service job, the client’s billing address and the work location may also be different. Use the fields available in the interface and include any additional job-location details in the description or notes where appropriate.

Check spelling, address details, and tax identifiers before issuing the invoice. Incorrect client details can create confusion and may require a corrected document.

## 5. Invoice Metadata & Terms

Invoice metadata helps identify the document and communicate when payment is expected.

### Available fields

- Invoice Number
- Invoice Date
- Payment Terms
- Due Date
- Purchase Order Number (optional)

### Understanding these fields

**Invoice Number:** A reference used to identify an invoice. Use a consistent numbering approach and avoid accidentally assigning the same number to different invoices when your recordkeeping process requires unique identifiers.

**Invoice Date:** The date shown on the invoice. Check that it reflects the appropriate date for the transaction.

**Payment Terms:** The agreed payment expectation, such as payment on receipt or another agreed period. Use wording that accurately reflects the arrangement with the client.

**Due Date:** The date by which payment is expected according to the stated terms. Check that it is consistent with your agreement and invoice date.

**Purchase Order Number:** An optional client reference that may help a business match the invoice to an approved purchase order.

### Before saving

- Check that the invoice number is correct.
- Verify the invoice date and due date.
- Make sure the payment terms are clear and consistent with the agreement.
- Add a purchase order reference when the client requires it.
- Avoid treating the invoice status as proof that payment has actually been received; update status based on your records.

The interface may allow additional line items as needed. The exact list and field behaviour can depend on the version.

### How to enter a line item

1. Enter a clear product, service, or work description.
2. Select an appropriate unit.
3. Enter the quantity.
4. Enter the agreed rate per unit.
5. Apply an item-level discount if relevant.
6. Enter the applicable tax rate when required and supported.
7. Review the line total before moving to the next item.
8. Add another line for a separate product, service, or charge when appropriate.

### Choosing a useful description

Avoid vague descriptions when more detail is helpful. For example, “Electrical repair service” may be less informative than “Electrical fault inspection and switch replacement,” provided that the more specific description accurately represents the work completed.

For product sales, include the product name and relevant details. For service work, describe the work performed, service period, or job reference as appropriate. Do not include unnecessary personal information.

### HSN / SAC / S field

The source interface describes this field as HSN / SAC / S, where applicable. Use the correct classification for the item or service when required. Do not guess a classification just to complete the field; verify it using an appropriate official or professional source if uncertain.

### Quantity, unit, and rate

Check that the selected unit matches the rate. For example, if the rate is charged per hour, the quantity should represent the number of hours. A mismatch between the unit and rate can lead to an incorrect total.

## 7. Understanding Line Totals and Invoice Totals

The invoice generator calculates line totals and displays a subtotal and grand total. Always review the calculation before issuing the document.

A simplified illustration of an item calculation is:

**Line amount before adjustments = Quantity × Rate**

For example, if a service is billed at ₹500 per hour for 3 hours:

- Quantity: 3 hours
- Rate: ₹500 per hour
- Amount before any discount or tax: ₹1,500

If a discount or tax applies, the final line amount depends on how the invoice tool applies those fields. Review the displayed calculation rather than assuming a particular order of operations.

The invoice-level total may also be affected by overall discounts and shipping or delivery charges. The exact treatment of taxes, discounts, and rounding can depend on the configuration and rules applicable to the transaction.

### Review these values

- Quantity multiplied by rate
- Item-level discounts
- Tax rate for each line, when applicable
- Subtotal
- Overall discount
- Shipping or delivery charges
- Grand total
- Amount in words, if shown

If the displayed total looks unexpected, check the line items and adjustments one by one before saving or sharing the invoice.

## 8. Additional Discounts & Charges

The invoice generator provides fields for invoice-level adjustments, including:

- Overall Discount
- Shipping / Delivery Charges
- Amount in Words

### Overall discount

An overall discount applies to the invoice as a whole, according to the tool’s implementation. Confirm whether the field expects a percentage or a fixed amount before entering a value. Do not assume the calculation method if the interface does not make it clear.

### Shipping or delivery charges

Use this field when a delivery or shipping charge applies to the transaction. Enter only the amount agreed with the client and review how it affects the total.

### Amount in words

An amount in words can make the payable amount easier to read on a printed invoice. Check that it matches the displayed grand total, especially after changing discounts, tax, or additional charges.

### Example for review

Suppose a service line is ₹1,500 before adjustments and a separate agreed delivery or visit charge is ₹200. The amount before any other applicable discounts or taxes would be ₹1,700. This is a simple illustration, not a statement about the tool’s exact tax order or rounding method.

## 9. Payment Information & UPI QR Code

Payment details tell the client how to pay the invoice.

### Available fields

- Bank Name
- Account Holder Name
- Account Number / IBAN
- IFSC / SWIFT / Routing Code
- Branch / Country
- UPI ID / Payment Link

Entering supported UPI or payment-link information enables the payment QR code feature for the printed invoice.

### Safe setup and review

1. Enter only the payment details you intend to share with the client.
2. Check the account holder name and relevant bank identifiers carefully.
3. If you use a UPI ID or payment link, verify it before creating the final invoice.
4. If a QR code is generated, test it using a safe method before sharing the invoice.
5. Ensure that the QR code or payment link points to the intended payment destination.
6. Review the printed or exported invoice to confirm the details are legible.

Never assume that a QR code is correct merely because it appears on the invoice. Verify the destination before sending the invoice to a client. Do not include passwords, PINs, OTPs, or other authentication secrets in payment details.

## 11. Invoice Status

The interface lists the following status options:

- Draft
- Unpaid
- Paid
- Overdue

Use the status that reflects the current state of the invoice according to your records.

**Draft:** The invoice is still being prepared or reviewed.

**Unpaid:** The invoice has been issued or recorded, but payment has not been confirmed.

**Paid:** Payment has been received and verified according to your records.

**Overdue:** Payment has not been received by the due date, based on the terms and dates recorded.

The status label helps organize invoices, but it does not itself verify a bank transfer or payment. Confirm payment using your actual payment records before marking an invoice as paid. If the interface does not automatically update overdue status, review the due date and status manually.

## 14. How to Generate an Invoice

Follow this workflow for a new invoice. Field names and exact controls may vary by version.

### Step 1: Enter business details

Enter the sender’s business information or select previously saved details. Check the business name, address, contact details, logo, and tax registration information where applicable.

### Step 2: Enter client details

Add the client’s name, billing address, and other relevant information. Use a separate shipping address if the transaction requires one and the interface provides that field.

### Step 3: Configure invoice information

Set the invoice number, invoice date, payment terms, due date, currency, and status. Add a purchase order number if the client requires it.

### Step 4: Add products or services

Enter the description, quantity, unit, rate, item-level discount, and applicable tax rate for each line. Add additional line items as required.

### Step 5: Review discounts, charges, and totals

Check the subtotal, overall discount, delivery charges, tax fields, grand total, and amount in words if available. Recalculate an example independently if a figure looks unusual.

### Step 6: Add payment information

Enter relevant bank details, a UPI ID, or a payment link if you intend to include them. Check the details carefully before sharing the invoice.

### Step 7: Review terms and conditions

Add client notes, payment conditions, authorized signatory information, and a signature image if appropriate and supported.

### Step 8: Preview and proofread

Use the available **Preview & Print** option. Review the complete invoice for missing fields, spelling errors, cut-off content, incorrect dates, and totals that do not match your records.

### Step 9: Save the invoice

Use **Save Invoice** if available in the interface. Confirm that the invoice was saved successfully. Do not assume that previewing or printing automatically saves a copy.

### Step 10: Print or export

Use the available interface options to print or prepare a PDF copy. Open the final file and check its pages, readability, totals, logo, and payment information before sending it to the client.

## 15. GST & Non-GST Invoicing

The invoice generator supports invoice preparation with configurable tax fields. Users should enter tax information according to their business situation and the requirements applicable to the transaction.

### Tax fields are not a compliance guarantee

Having a tax field or a tax-oriented template does not automatically mean that the invoice is compliant with every current GST rule. Requirements can depend on the supplier, recipient, goods or services, transaction type, registration status, location, and applicable law.

Before issuing an invoice, verify the applicable tax rate, tax calculation, registration details, required invoice particulars, and any other conditions relevant to your transaction.

### Practical review

- Confirm whether GST or another tax applies to the transaction.
- Verify the applicable rate and classification using a suitable official or professional source.
- Check the supplier and recipient details.
- Verify the invoice number and date.
- Check the tax amounts and grand total.
- Ensure that the final document contains the information required for the type of invoice you are issuing.

If you are uncertain about the correct tax treatment, consult a qualified accountant or tax professional. Do not guess a tax rate or classification merely to complete a field.

**Important:** Daily Khata Pro helps with invoice preparation and calculations. Users remain responsible for ensuring that their invoices comply with applicable tax, accounting, and legal requirements.

## 16. Saving, Printing, and Keeping Invoice Records

A saved invoice, a printed invoice, a PDF export, and an app backup may serve different purposes.

- **Saved invoice:** A record retained by the application, depending on its storage implementation.
- **Printed invoice:** A physical copy of the document.
- **PDF copy:** A shareable or archivable representation of the invoice.
- **App backup:** A backup of data included by the app’s backup feature; it should not be assumed to contain every PDF or attachment unless the feature documentation confirms this.

After issuing an invoice, keep a copy according to your business recordkeeping process. Use clear filenames when saving PDFs, for example by invoice number and date, while avoiding unnecessary exposure of client details in filenames.

If the application stores invoice records locally, changes to browser storage or moving to another device may affect access to those records. Use the available backup process and verify important records separately.

## 17. Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Leaving the client name or invoice number incorrect.
- Reusing an invoice number unintentionally.
- Entering a quantity with a rate that uses a different unit.
- Applying a discount without checking whether the field expects a percentage or fixed amount.
- Entering a tax rate or classification without verifying it.
- Forgetting to review shipping charges or other adjustments.
- Assuming currency selection performs currency conversion.
- Marking an invoice as paid before confirming receipt of payment.
- Sharing an invoice before checking the bank details, UPI ID, payment link, or QR code.
- Assuming the app’s invoice template automatically satisfies all legal requirements.
- Assuming a preview or print action automatically saves the invoice.
- Failing to open the final PDF or printout and check the layout.

## 18. Troubleshooting and Review Tips

### The total looks incorrect

Review quantity, rate, unit, item-level discounts, tax fields, overall discount, and additional charges. Check whether the discount field expects a percentage or a fixed amount. If the order of tax and discount calculations is not clear, use the displayed result cautiously and verify it independently.

### A business or client detail is wrong

Return to the relevant section and correct the information before issuing the invoice. If the invoice has already been sent, follow your business process and applicable rules for correcting or replacing the document.

### The logo or signature does not appear as expected

Check the selected image file, supported format, and layout preview. The source describes PNG/JPG for signature images; exact size limits and image-handling behaviour may depend on the version.

### The PDF or print layout is incomplete

Preview the final document and inspect all pages. Long descriptions, many line items, logos, or notes can affect layout. Try a supported template or adjust the content if the interface allows it, then preview again.

### The QR code or payment link looks incorrect

Do not send the invoice until you verify the payment destination. Check the entered UPI ID or payment link and confirm that the QR code directs to the intended recipient using a safe verification method.

### A saved invoice cannot be found

Check the app’s available invoice list or records area, filters, and the storage or backup workflow described by the current version. Do not clear site data or reinstall the app before considering whether the invoice exists only in local storage and whether a backup is available.

Exact controls may vary by application version. If the problem persists, document the steps and contact the project’s official support channel without sharing sensitive financial information.

## 19. Privacy and Payment Safety

Invoices can contain names, addresses, phone numbers, tax identifiers, bank details, and transaction information. Share them only with intended recipients.

Recommended practices:

- Check the recipient before sending a PDF or printed invoice.
- Verify payment information and QR codes before sharing.
- Do not include passwords, PINs, OTPs, or account login credentials.
- Avoid uploading invoices to public links or public repositories.
- Store invoice copies and backups in a location with appropriate access controls.
- Use sample data when testing unfamiliar builds or invoice functions.
- Review screenshots and bug reports for private client and payment information.

A PDF or image of an invoice may be readable by anyone who obtains a copy. Do not assume that a generated invoice is encrypted or access-controlled unless the actual sharing and storage method provides those protections.
