# Chapter 4: The Smart Fund Allocation Rule

## Overview

The **Smart Fund Allocation** feature is intended to help users divide
an available amount into multiple planned categories using
percentage-based rules. It can be useful for organizing a budget,
planning how income might be distributed, or creating a simple reference
for how much of an amount is assigned to different purposes.

For example, a user may want to plan how an amount is divided between
regular expenses, savings, business needs, and an emergency reserve. A
percentage-based allocation can calculate the suggested amount for each
category from a total amount entered by the user.

> **Important:** Fund allocation is a planning and calculation aid. It
> does not automatically transfer money between bank accounts, create
> savings accounts, make investments, or guarantee financial results
> unless a separate feature explicitly provides and documents such an
> action.

> **Version note:** The exact controls, category names, saving behavior,
> and available settings may differ between Daily Khata Pro versions.
> Follow the interface and instructions available in the installed
> version.

## 4.1 Purpose of Smart Fund Allocation

Smart Fund Allocation is designed to make percentage-based planning
easier and more consistent.

It can help users:

-   Divide a total amount into planned categories.
-   Calculate a suggested amount for each category.
-   Review how a chosen allocation rule distributes an amount.
-   Adjust percentages when personal or business priorities change.
-   Compare different budgeting scenarios.
-   Reduce repeated manual calculations.

The feature does not determine what a user should spend, save, invest,
or reserve. Users decide which categories to use and which percentages
are appropriate for their circumstances.

## 4.2 Understanding the Basic Rule

An allocation rule uses a total amount and a percentage for each
category.

The basic formula is:

**Allocated amount = Total amount × Allocation percentage ÷ 100**

For example, suppose the amount available for planning is **₹10,000**
and the user configures the following example allocation:

  Example category           Percentage Calculation            Allocated amount
  ------------------------ ------------ -------------------- ------------------
  Regular expenses                  50% ₹10,000 × 50 ÷ 100               ₹5,000
  Savings                           20% ₹10,000 × 20 ÷ 100               ₹2,000
  Business or work needs            20% ₹10,000 × 20 ÷ 100               ₹2,000
  Emergency reserve                 10% ₹10,000 × 10 ÷ 100               ₹1,000
  **Total**                    **100%**                             **₹10,000**

These percentages are only an illustration. They are not a
recommendation and may not suit every person's income, expenses,
obligations, or financial goals.

## 4.3 Why the Total Percentage Matters

For a complete allocation of the entered amount, the configured
percentages should generally total **100%**, if the application requires
a full allocation.

### When percentages total 100%

The entire entered amount is assigned across the configured categories.

Example: 50% + 30% + 20% = 100%.

### When percentages total less than 100%

Some of the amount remains unallocated under the example rule.

Example: 50% + 30% + 10% = 90%. For ₹10,000, the categories receive
₹5,000, ₹3,000, and ₹1,000, totaling ₹9,000. The remaining ₹1,000 is not
assigned by those percentages.

### When percentages total more than 100%

The requested allocations exceed the original amount.

Example: 60% + 30% + 20% = 110%. For ₹10,000, the calculated category
amounts total ₹11,000. This exceeds the entered amount by ₹1,000.

If the app validates allocation percentages, follow the message shown by
the interface. If it does not enforce a total, check the sum yourself
before relying on the results.

## 4.4 Choosing Categories

A category represents a purpose to which a portion of the total amount
is assigned. Depending on the app's implementation, categories may be
predefined, editable, or configurable.

Possible example categories include:

-   Regular household or personal expenses.
-   Savings.
-   Emergency reserve.
-   Business or work-related needs.
-   Education or training.
-   Planned purchases.
-   Other user-defined purposes.

These are examples, not a statement that every category is built into
the app.

Choose categories that are meaningful to you. Avoid creating overlapping
categories that describe the same expense, because overlapping
categories can make a plan difficult to interpret.

For example, if "Household expenses" already includes groceries, do not
also count the same grocery amount under a separate category unless the
allocation rule intentionally treats them as separate planned amounts.

## 4.5 Setting or Updating Allocation Percentages

If the installed version provides controls for editing the allocation
rule, use the following general process:

1.  Open the Smart Fund Allocation tool or its settings.
2.  Review the existing category names and percentages.
3.  Decide what the rule is intended to represent, such as a monthly
    income plan or a one-time budget.
4.  Enter the percentage for each category using the available fields.
5.  Add, remove, or rename categories only if the interface supports
    those actions.
6.  Check the total of all percentages.
7.  Save or confirm the changes if the app provides a save action.
8.  Test the rule with a small example amount.
9.  Review the results and correct any configuration errors.

Do not assume that a changed percentage has been saved automatically.
Check for a confirmation message or reopen the settings to verify the
saved values if appropriate.

## 4.6 Calculating an Allocation

The precise steps depend on the current interface, but a typical
calculation workflow is:

1.  Open the Smart Fund Allocation feature.
2.  Enter the total amount you want to plan.
3.  Review the configured categories and percentages.
4.  Check that the percentages are appropriate for this calculation.
5.  Run or view the calculation using the available control.
6.  Review each category's percentage and calculated amount.
7.  Check the total allocated amount against the original amount.
8.  Save, export, or print the result only if those functions are
    supported and needed.

### Example

Suppose the amount entered is **₹25,000** and the example rule is:

-   40% for regular expenses.
-   25% for savings.
-   20% for work or business needs.
-   15% for an emergency reserve.

The calculations are:

  Category                   Percentage   Calculated amount
  ------------------------ ------------ -------------------
  Regular expenses                  40%             ₹10,000
  Savings                           25%              ₹6,250
  Work or business needs            20%              ₹5,000
  Emergency reserve                 15%              ₹3,750
  **Total**                    **100%**         **₹25,000**

This example demonstrates the arithmetic only. It does not indicate that
these percentages are suitable for any particular user.

## 4.7 Using Allocation for Different Planning Scenarios

A percentage rule can be applied to different amounts to explore how a
plan changes. The user should define what the total amount represents
before interpreting the result.

### Monthly income planning

A user might enter an amount representing money received during a month
and calculate a proposed distribution across planned categories.

Remember to account for actual obligations, irregular expenses, taxes,
debt payments, and other commitments when creating a real budget. The
allocation output does not automatically know about these obligations
unless they are represented in the inputs or the app's supported data.

### One-time amount planning

A user might enter a one-time amount, such as a project payment or a
planned purchase budget, and see how the selected percentages divide
that amount.

Do not apply a monthly allocation rule to a one-time amount without
considering whether the rule is appropriate for that purpose.

### Comparing alternative rules

A user can compare different percentage configurations if the app allows
the rule to be changed and recalculated. For example, one scenario might
allocate a larger share to a planned purchase, while another assigns
more to a reserve.

Treat comparisons as planning exercises. The results do not predict
future income or expenses.

## 4.8 Understanding Results and Rounding

Allocation results are calculated from the total amount and configured
percentages. For currency amounts, decimal precision and rounding may
affect displayed values, especially when percentages produce fractions
of the smallest currency unit.

When reviewing a result:

-   Check the original total.
-   Check each percentage.
-   Check each calculated amount.
-   Add the displayed amounts together.
-   Look for any unallocated or excess amount.
-   Review how the app handles decimal places and rounding, if shown.

For example, if individual results are rounded, the displayed category
amounts may differ slightly from a total calculated using unrounded
values. Follow the app's displayed precision and verify important
amounts independently.

## 4.9 Allocation Is Not a Transaction

A key distinction is the difference between a **planned allocation** and
an **actual financial transaction**.

A planned allocation indicates how the user intends to divide an amount.
It does not necessarily mean that the money has been moved, spent,
deposited, or invested.

Examples:

-   Allocating ₹2,000 to "Savings" does not prove that ₹2,000 has been
    deposited into a bank account.
-   Allocating ₹1,000 to "Business expenses" does not create an expense
    transaction.
-   Allocating an amount to an "Emergency reserve" does not
    automatically open a separate account or restrict access to that
    money.

If the app separately provides transaction or account features, use
those features to record actual activity according to their
instructions.

## 4.10 Relationship to Income, Expenses, Goals, and Reports

Smart Fund Allocation may complement other financial tools, but users
should not assume that every feature is automatically linked to every
other feature.

### Income records

An income record documents money received. An allocation plan can be
used to explore how an amount of income might be divided, but the plan
does not necessarily create or modify an income record.

### Expense records

An expense record documents a financial outflow. An allocation for
planned expenses is not the same as an expense that has actually
occurred.

### Financial goals

An allocation may help a user think about how much to set aside toward a
goal. Unless the app explicitly documents automatic integration, do not
assume that an allocation updates goal progress.

### Reports

A report may summarize records stored in the app. Do not assume that a
planning calculation appears in financial reports unless the current
version explicitly supports that behavior.

For important tracking, verify the relevant section after performing an
action.

## 4.11 Practical Example: Planning a Project Payment

**Situation:** A user receives ₹18,000 for a completed project and wants
to explore a possible distribution.

For illustration, the user sets the following categories:

-   45% for regular expenses.
-   25% for savings.
-   20% for business costs.
-   10% for a reserve.

The calculated amounts would be:

  Category             Percentage        Amount
  ------------------ ------------ -------------
  Regular expenses            45%        ₹8,100
  Savings                     25%        ₹4,500
  Business costs              20%        ₹3,600
  Reserve                     10%        ₹1,800
  **Total**              **100%**   **₹18,000**

Before treating this as a real budget, the user should consider taxes,
existing commitments, upcoming bills, and any other obligations that may
apply. The example is not personal financial advice.

## 4.12 Practical Example: Finding an Incorrect Allocation

**Situation:** A user enters ₹12,000 but the category amounts add up to
₹13,200.

A total of ₹13,200 represents 110% of ₹12,000. The user should:

1.  Reopen the allocation rule.
2.  Check each configured percentage.
3.  Add the percentages together.
4.  Identify whether the total exceeds 100%.
5.  Correct the rule if a full allocation of the original amount is
    intended.
6.  Recalculate and check the result.

If the displayed amounts do not match the expected arithmetic even
though the percentages total 100%, check the entered amount, displayed
rounding, and any additional rules shown by the app.

## 4.13 Troubleshooting

### The allocated amounts do not equal the entered amount

Check whether the percentages total 100%, whether any categories were
omitted, and whether displayed amounts are rounded.

### The percentages total less than 100%

The rule assigns less than the full amount. If the intention is to
allocate the whole amount, review the percentages and adjust them as
appropriate.

### The percentages total more than 100%

The calculated category amounts exceed the original total. Review the
configuration before relying on the results.

### A category or percentage cannot be edited

The current version may use fixed categories or may place configuration
controls in another section. Follow the controls that are actually
available rather than assuming all fields are editable.

### Changes do not appear in the result

Check whether the settings were saved, whether the calculation needs to
be run again, and whether the result is using the intended amount or
rule.

### The allocation does not appear in a report

Planning calculations and financial records may be separate. Check the
relevant documentation to confirm whether the app supports including
allocation results in reports.

## 4.14 Privacy and Data Handling

Allocation amounts and category labels can reveal information about a
user's personal or business finances. Protect the device and any
exported results appropriately.

If the app stores allocation settings locally, changes to browser or
application storage may affect those settings, depending on the
implementation. Do not assume that the rule is automatically
synchronized to another device or included in every backup unless the
installed version documents that behavior.

Before clearing local data or changing devices, review the app's backup
and restoration instructions.

## 4.15 Limitations

Smart Fund Allocation should be understood as a budgeting and
calculation aid. Depending on the version, the feature may have
limitations such as:

-   It may not automatically move money between accounts.
-   It may not verify the user's actual income or expenses.
-   It may not account for obligations that were not entered.
-   It may not automatically update financial goals or transaction
    records.
-   It may not appear in reports unless explicitly supported.
-   Results depend on the amount and percentages entered.
-   Displayed results may be affected by currency precision or rounding.
-   It does not guarantee that a budget is realistic or that a financial
    goal will be achieved.

## 4.16 Important Disclaimer

The Smart Fund Allocation feature is intended for financial organization
and planning. Example percentages in this chapter are illustrative only
and should not be interpreted as a recommendation.

Users are responsible for choosing suitable categories, entering
accurate amounts, reviewing the calculations, and considering their
actual obligations and circumstances. The feature does not guarantee
savings, investment returns, financial stability, or any other outcome.

For consequential financial, tax, lending, or investment decisions,
verify the information independently and consult a qualified
professional when appropriate.

## 4.18 Chapter Summary

Smart Fund Allocation helps users calculate how an amount can be divided
across configured categories using percentages. The main formula is:

**Allocated amount = Total amount × Percentage ÷ 100**

For a complete allocation, percentages generally need to total 100%.
Users should review the arithmetic, understand that the output is a plan
rather than a money transfer, and record actual transactions separately
where appropriate.

Use the feature as an organizational aid, verify important results, and
refer to the installed version's controls and instructions for exact
workflows.
