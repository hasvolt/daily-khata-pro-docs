# Chapter 9: Split Bill & Group Share

## 9.1 Overview

The **Split Bill & Group Share** feature is intended to help users
calculate how a shared expense can be divided among friends, family
members, colleagues, or other participants. Shared expenses commonly
arise during meals, travel, accommodation, household purchases, events,
group shopping, or work-related activities.

A clear calculation helps participants understand the amount associated
with each person's share. It can reduce confusion when a group needs to
settle a bill, compare contributions, or keep a record of an agreed
expense.

The exact options available depend on the installed version of Daily
Khata Pro. This chapter explains the underlying concepts, calculation
methods, practical workflow, common checks, and record-keeping
considerations. Follow only the controls that are actually present in
your version of the application.

**Important distinction:** A split calculation is not the same as a
payment. It does not, by itself, transfer money, confirm that a
participant has paid, create a debt, or settle a shared expense unless
the application explicitly provides and confirms such functionality.

## 9.2 What Is Bill Splitting?

Bill splitting means dividing one agreed total expense between two or
more participants using a chosen method.

For example, four people may agree to share a ₹1,200 meal equally. Each
person's calculated share would be ₹300. In another situation, one
participant may pay a larger portion, or the group may assign custom
amounts based on what each person used.

A bill-splitting calculation is useful for: - Understanding each
participant's expected share. - Checking whether assigned amounts match
the total bill. - Discussing how a shared cost should be divided. -
Reviewing amounts before collecting or reimbursing money. - Supporting
personal financial record-keeping.

The calculation does not independently determine what is fair.
Participants should agree on the method and included items before
relying on the result.

## 9.3 Common Ways to Divide a Bill

### 9.3.1 Equal split

An equal split divides the total bill evenly among all participants.

**Formula:**

`Individual share = Total bill ÷ Number of participants`

**Example:**

-   Total bill: ₹1,200
-   Participants: 4
-   Share per person: ₹1,200 ÷ 4 = ₹300

Each participant's calculated share is ₹300.

Equal splitting is straightforward when everyone agrees to contribute
the same amount. It may not be appropriate when participants used
different services or agreed to pay different portions.

### 9.3.2 Custom amount split

If the tool supports custom amounts, assign a specific amount to each
participant instead of dividing the bill equally.

**Example:**

A group has a total bill of ₹2,000 and agrees to these shares:

  Participant       Agreed share
  --------------- --------------
  Participant A             ₹800
  Participant B             ₹700
  Participant C             ₹500
  **Total**           **₹2,000**

Before accepting a custom split, add the assigned amounts together. If
the sum is lower than the bill, some amount remains unallocated. If the
sum is higher, the group has allocated more than the agreed total.

Custom amounts should reflect the arrangement agreed by the
participants, not an assumption made by the calculator.

### 9.3.3 Percentage-based split

If percentage splitting is available, each participant receives a
defined percentage of the total.

**Formula:**

`Participant share = Total bill × Percentage ÷ 100`

**Example:**

For a ₹2,500 expense:

-   Participant A: 60% = ₹2,500 × 60 ÷ 100 = ₹1,500
-   Participant B: 40% = ₹2,500 × 40 ÷ 100 = ₹1,000
-   Total shares: ₹2,500

The percentages should add up to **100%**. If they do not, review the
entries before relying on the result.

## 9.4 Understand the Total Bill Before Splitting

Enter the total amount that the group has agreed to divide. Review the
receipt or bill first so the entered amount represents the correct
total.

Depending on the arrangement, the total may include: - Item or service
prices. - Taxes already shown on the bill. - Agreed service, delivery,
or booking charges. - A tip, but only if the group agrees to include
it. - Other fees that the group has decided to share.

Avoid counting the same amount twice. For example, if the final bill
already includes tax, do not add that tax again as a separate amount.

### When one item belongs to only one participant

Sometimes a bill includes a shared portion and an item used by only one
person. If the application supports custom amounts or another suitable
split method, the group may calculate the individual item separately and
divide the remaining shared amount among the other participants.

If the available tool cannot represent the agreed arrangement, calculate
the portions separately and clearly record how the final amounts were
determined. Do not assume that the app supports item-level splitting
unless that control is actually available.

## 9.5 General Workflow for Using a Split-Bill Tool

The interface can vary by version. If your installed version provides a
split-bill tool, use the following general workflow.

1.  **Open the relevant tool.** Locate the split-bill feature in the
    application, if available.
2.  **Confirm the bill total.** Check the receipt and enter the agreed
    total accurately.
3.  **Choose a split method.** Select equal shares, custom amounts, or
    percentages only if that method is provided.
4.  **Enter participant information or share values.** Use only the
    fields available in your version.
5.  **Review all values.** Check the total, participant count,
    percentages, and custom amounts.
6.  **Check the sum of shares.** The final shares should match the
    agreed total, allowing only for a clearly handled rounding
    difference.
7.  **Review the result.** Make sure the displayed amount for each
    participant is understandable and consistent with the agreed method.
8.  **Save or record the result, if supported.** Before saving, review
    the record type, date, category, and amount.
9.  **Confirm any saved record.** A calculated share is not proof that
    money has changed hands.

Not every control or step listed here will necessarily appear in every
version. If an option is not visible, do not assume it exists; use the
installed application's actual interface and current instructions.

## 9.6 Example: Equal Split

Suppose three friends share a meal costing ₹1,500.

  Detail                         Amount
  ------------------------ ------------
  Total bill                     ₹1,500
  Number of participants              3
  Calculation                ₹1,500 ÷ 3
  Share per participant            ₹500

Each participant's calculated share is ₹500.

This example demonstrates the arithmetic only. It does not indicate who
paid the restaurant, whether another participant reimbursed the payer,
or whether any amount has been recorded in Daily Khata Pro.

## 9.7 Example: Percentage Split

Suppose two participants agree to divide a ₹2,500 shared expense in a
60:40 ratio.

  Participant       Percentage Calculation                Share
  --------------- ------------ ------------------- ------------
  Participant A            60% ₹2,500 × 60 ÷ 100         ₹1,500
  Participant B            40% ₹2,500 × 40 ÷ 100         ₹1,000
  **Total**           **100%**                       **₹2,500**

The percentages total 100%, and the calculated shares match the bill.
Before using a percentage-based result, verify both the percentages and
the final amounts.

## 9.8 Rounding and Small Currency Differences

Some totals cannot be divided evenly into the smallest currency unit. A
calculation may produce fractions of a rupee, or the application may
round each share for display.

For example, ₹100 divided equally among three participants gives
approximately ₹33.333... per person. Three shares rounded to ₹33.33
total ₹99.99, leaving a difference of ₹0.01.

When a rounding difference appears: - Confirm the original total and
number of participants. - Check the displayed decimal precision. -
Review whether the application rounds each share or the final total. -
Agree how to assign any remaining paise. - Make sure the final agreed
shares add up to the bill.

Do not silently ignore a difference. Explain the rounding method to the
participants so everyone understands the final amounts.

## 9.9 Split Calculation Versus Expense Record

A **split calculation** estimates each participant's share. An **expense
record** documents a transaction entered into the application's
financial records. These are different activities.

If the application provides an option to save a split result to records,
review the following before saving:

-   **Amount:** Is the amount the one you intend to record?
-   **Date:** Is the correct transaction date selected, if the date
    field is available?
-   **Category or fund:** Is the selected category appropriate, if this
    option is available?
-   **Description:** Does the record make it clear what the expense was
    for?
-   **Duplicate check:** Has the same purchase already been recorded
    elsewhere?
-   **Actual versus planned:** Does the entry represent something that
    actually happened, rather than only a proposed payment?

Do not assume that calculating a split automatically adds an expense
record. Check whether the installed version provides a separate save or
add-to-records option.

### Avoid recording the same purchase more than once

Suppose one person pays the full restaurant bill and the other
participants reimburse that person later. The restaurant purchase and
the later reimbursements are related but are not necessarily separate
purchases of the same goods or services.

When maintaining records, avoid recording the full purchase multiple
times as if every reimbursement were a new expense. Use the record types
available in the application carefully and keep a clear note of the
arrangement when helpful.

The appropriate record-keeping approach depends on how you maintain your
finances and on the features available in the installed version.

## 9.10 Shared Expenses and Payment Status

A calculated share does not establish whether a participant has paid.

For example, a result may show that each person owes a calculated share,
but it does not confirm that the money was collected. Payment
confirmation must come from the actual transaction or another reliable
record.

If the application does not provide explicit payment-status tracking: -
Keep payment confirmations separately. - Compare the agreed shares with
actual payments and reimbursements. - Avoid marking an amount as settled
merely because the calculation is complete. - Do not assume the app
automatically sends payment requests or reminders.

Before treating a group expense as settled, verify the real payments and
any remaining balance between participants.

## 9.11 Reviewing, Correcting, or Recalculating a Split

If a calculation looks incorrect, review the original inputs before
changing records.

Check: 1. The total bill amount. 2. The number of participants. 3. The
selected split method. 4. Each custom amount or percentage. 5. Taxes,
fees, tips, and excluded items. 6. Rounding or decimal precision. 7.
Whether the displayed result is only a calculation or has also been
saved as a record.

If an incorrect result has been saved and editing is supported, use the
available correction controls. Review the resulting record after making
a change. Avoid creating a second entry to correct an existing one
unless the application's workflow specifically requires that approach.

## 9.12 Privacy and Sensitive Information

Group-expense records may reveal where people went, what they purchased,
or how they share costs. Enter only the information needed for the
calculation or record.

Good privacy practices include: - Avoid placing unnecessary personal or
payment details in notes. - Check reports before exporting, printing, or
sharing them. - Avoid sharing screenshots that expose private financial
information. - Protect access to your device and application. - Follow
the application's current backup and privacy guidance.

Anyone who can access an unlocked device or accessible application data
may be able to view information stored there. Do not assume that a
passcode or private display feature encrypts every record or protects
exported copies.

## 9.13 Common Problems and Troubleshooting

### The total does not match the shares

Check the total amount, participant count, custom amounts, and
percentages. For percentage splits, verify that the percentages add up
to 100%. For custom amounts, add all shares and compare their sum with
the bill.

### A participant's share appears incorrect

Confirm the split method and recheck all entered values. Verify that the
total includes only the items, taxes, fees, and tips the group agreed to
share.

### A small difference appears after division

This may be caused by rounding. Review the displayed decimal precision
and agree how to handle any remaining paise.

### The calculation is complete, but no record appears in Records

A calculation may not automatically create a financial record. Check
whether your installed version provides a separate save or
add-to-records option.

### The same expense appears more than once

Review the relevant entries and determine whether the same purchase was
recorded in multiple places. Correct duplicates only after confirming
which record should remain. If you are unsure, preserve the information
until you understand the effect of deleting or editing an entry.

### The group disagrees about the split

The calculator can perform arithmetic, but it cannot decide what
arrangement participants should accept. Agree on the included items and
split method first, then enter the agreed values.

### A feature described in this chapter is not visible

Available controls may differ by application version. Use the installed
version's actual interface and avoid assuming that an unavailable option
is supported.

## 9.14 Good Practices for Group Expenses

-   Agree on the split method before collecting money.
-   Use the final bill total and avoid double-counting taxes or fees.
-   Decide how to handle individual items before calculating.
-   Check that custom amounts or percentages match the agreed total.
-   Keep calculations separate from confirmed payments.
-   Review records before adding a transaction.
-   Avoid recording the same expense more than once.
-   Verify important amounts against the receipt and with participants.
-   Explain rounding differences clearly.
-   Keep shared financial information private.

## 9.15 Important Notice

Daily Khata Pro's split-bill calculations, where available, are intended
to support everyday calculations and financial record-keeping. A
calculated amount is not proof of payment, a payment request, or a
guarantee that participants will reimburse one another.

Users are responsible for verifying bill amounts, agreeing on the split
method, checking rounding differences, and confirming actual payments.
Available functionality may vary by application version. This chapter
explains general usage concepts and does not replace the application's
current interface or instructions.
